## 1. Motivation

Later lessons multiply Jacobians across time or position. A transpose, shape, or finite-difference mistake in this first module would therefore contaminate every later conclusion about vanishing gradients, additive memory, attention, or state-space recurrence. This lesson fixes the notation and builds the float64 harness that turns each derivative into a runnable numerical claim.

## 2. Forward definition

Vectors are columns. A vector $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ has shape $(d,)$, a matrix $W\in\mathbb{R}^{o\times i}$ has shape $(o,i)$, and a scalar loss $L\in\mathbb{R}$ has shape $()$. For $y=f(x)$, the Jacobian is

$$
J=\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}\in\mathbb{R}^{|y|\times |x|},
\qquad
\nabla_x L=J^\top\nabla_y L.
$$

The shared harness evaluates a scalar function $f(\mathrm{params})\in\mathbb{R}$ and compares an analytic gradient with the central difference

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_r}
\approx
\frac{f(p+\varepsilon e_r)-f(p-\varepsilon e_r)}{2\varepsilon},
\qquad \varepsilon=10^{-6}.
$$

All check inputs and perturbations use float64. The harness reports maximum absolute error, maximum relative error, the worst index, a per-parameter table, and a Boolean pass result.

## 3. Derivations

### D-M0.1-01 · Softmax Jacobian

**Claim.** For $z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $p=\operatorname{softmax}(z)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, the Jacobian $J\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ is

$$
J=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top.
$$

**Steps.**

1. Define $a_i=\exp(z_i)$ and $Z=\sum_{k=1}^{d}a_k$, so $p_i=a_i/Z$. (This names the numerator and shared denominator before applying the quotient rule.)
2. Differentiate the numerator: $\partial a_i/\partial z_j=\delta_{ij}a_i$. (The Kronecker delta records whether the perturbed coordinate is the numerator coordinate.)
3. Differentiate the denominator: $\partial Z/\partial z_j=a_j$. (Only the $j$th exponential changes with $z_j$.)
4. Apply the quotient rule:
   $$
   \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}
   =\frac{\delta_{ij}a_iZ-a_i a_j}{Z^2}.
   $$
   (The first term differentiates the numerator and the second differentiates the denominator.)
5. Substitute $a_i/Z=p_i$ and $a_j/Z=p_j$:
   $$
   \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}=\delta_{ij}p_i-p_i p_j.
   $$
   (This expresses every entry using the output probabilities.)
6. Collect the entries into a matrix: $J=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top$. (The diagonal term contains $\delta_{ij}p_i$ and the outer product contains $p_i p_j$.)
7. $J$ is symmetric because both $\operatorname{diag}(p)$ and $pp^\top$ are symmetric. (Symmetry is visible term by term.)
8. $J\mathbf{1}=p-p(p^\top\mathbf{1})=p-p=0$. (The probabilities sum to one, so the all-ones direction is in the null space and every row sums to zero.)

**Check.** `checks/M0/D-M0.1-01.py` — `max_abs_err = 6.399e-10`, `max_rel_err = 1.939e-08`, `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** Increasing one logit raises its probability and removes probability from the other entries. The outer-product term is the normalization feedback that couples all coordinates.

### D-M0.1-02 · Softmax plus cross-entropy gradient

**Claim.** For target index $y\in\{1,\ldots,d\}$ and $L=-\log p_y$, the logit gradient is

$$
\nabla_z L=p-e_y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
$$

**Derivation through D-M0.1-01.**

1. The probability gradient has entries $\partial L/\partial p_i=-\delta_{iy}/p_y$. (Only the target probability appears in $-\log p_y$.)
2. Apply the transposed-Jacobian chain rule:
   $$
   \nabla_zL=J^\top\nabla_pL.
   $$
   (The Jacobian maps logit perturbations to probability perturbations, so reverse mode uses its transpose.)
3. Substitute $J_{ij}=\delta_{ij}p_i-p_i p_j$ and sum over $i$:
   $$
   \frac{\partial L}{\partial z_j}
   =\sum_i\left(-\frac{\delta_{iy}}{p_y}\right)(\delta_{ij}p_i-p_i p_j).
   $$
   (This writes the full reverse-mode contraction.)
4. Only $i=y$ survives the delta factor, so
   $$
   \frac{\partial L}{\partial z_j}
   =-\frac{1}{p_y}(\delta_{yj}p_y-p_y p_j)=p_j-\delta_{yj}.
   $$
   (The target probability cancels from both terms.)
5. Therefore $\nabla_zL=p-e_y$. (The vector form collects the coordinate result.)

**Direct derivation.**

1. Rewrite the loss using the softmax quotient:
   $$
   L=-\log\frac{\exp(z_y)}{\sum_k\exp(z_k)}
   =\log\sum_k\exp(z_k)-z_y.
   $$
   (Taking the logarithm turns the quotient into a difference.)
2. Differentiate the log-sum-exp term:
   $$
   \frac{\partial}{\partial z_j}\log\sum_k\exp(z_k)
   =\frac{\exp(z_j)}{\sum_k\exp(z_k)}=p_j.
   $$
   (The quotient is exactly the softmax coordinate.)
3. Differentiate $-z_y$: $\partial(-z_y)/\partial z_j=-\delta_{yj}$. (The target coordinate contributes one direct term.)
4. Add the two terms to obtain $\partial L/\partial z_j=p_j-\delta_{yj}$. (Both derivations produce the same vector.)

**Check.** `checks/M0/D-M0.1-02.py` — `max_abs_err = 1.561e-10`, `max_rel_err = 8.760e-10`, `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** The gradient asks the model to move probability mass toward the target: every non-target coordinate receives its current probability, while the target receives that probability minus one.

### D-M0.1-03 · LayerNorm Jacobian

**Claim.** For $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $\varepsilon>0$, $\mu=\frac1d\mathbf{1}^\top x$, $\sigma=\sqrt{\frac1d\|x-\mu\mathbf{1}\|^2+\varepsilon}$, and $\hat{x}=(x-\mu\mathbf{1})/\sigma$,

$$
\frac{\partial\hat{x}}{\partial x}
=\frac1\sigma\left(I-\frac1d\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top-\frac1d\hat{x}\hat{x}^\top\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.
$$

For $y=\gamma\odot\hat{x}+\beta$ with $\gamma,\beta,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$,

$$
\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}=\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)\frac{\partial\hat{x}}{\partial x},
\quad
\frac{\partial y}{\partial\gamma}=\operatorname{diag}(\hat{x}),
\quad
\frac{\partial y}{\partial\beta}=I.
$$

**Steps.**

1. Let $u=x-\mu\mathbf{1}$ and $P=I-\frac1d\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$. Then $u=Px$. (Subtracting the mean is a linear projection.)
2. Differentiate $u$: $du=P\,dx$. (The matrix $P$ is constant with respect to $x$.)
3. Write $\sigma=(u^\top u/d+\varepsilon)^{1/2}$. (This is the given standard deviation with stabilizing epsilon.)
4. Differentiate the square root:
   $$
   d\sigma=\frac{1}{2\sigma}\frac{2u^\top du}{d}=\frac{u^\top P\,dx}{d\sigma}.
   $$
   (The chain rule differentiates the quadratic and then the square root.)
5. Since $P$ is symmetric and $Pu=u$, $u^\top P=u^\top$. (The centered vector already lies in the mean-zero subspace.)
6. Differentiate $\hat{x}=u/\sigma$:
   $$
   d\hat{x}=\frac{du}{\sigma}-\frac{u}{\sigma^2}d\sigma
   =\left(\frac{P}{\sigma}-\frac{uu^\top}{d\sigma^3}\right)dx.
   $$
   (The first term differentiates the numerator and the second differentiates the reciprocal scale.)
7. Because $\hat{x}=u/\sigma$, $uu^\top/\sigma^2=\hat{x}\hat{x}^\top$. Therefore
   $$
   \frac{\partial\hat{x}}{\partial x}
   =\frac1\sigma\left(I-\frac1d\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top-\frac1d\hat{x}\hat{x}^\top\right).
   $$
   (Substitution converts the centered-vector term into the normalized-vector term.)
8. Differentiate $y=\gamma\odot\hat{x}+\beta$ coordinatewise to obtain the three displayed Jacobians. (Elementwise multiplication is a diagonal linear map when one factor is fixed.)

**Check.** `checks/M0/D-M0.1-03.py` — normalized-vector Jacobian `max_abs_err = 1.446e-10`, full affine loss gradient `max_abs_err = 2.450e-10`, both `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** LayerNorm removes the mean direction and rescales the centered direction. The affine parameters then restore a learned coordinatewise scale and offset.

### D-M0.1-04 · RMSNorm Jacobian

**Claim.** For $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $r=\sqrt{\frac1d x^\top x+\varepsilon}$, and $y=x/r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$,

$$
\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}
=\frac1r\left(I-\frac1d yy^\top\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.
$$

**Steps.**

1. Write $r=(x^\top x/d+\varepsilon)^{1/2}$. (This exposes the scalar whose derivative controls the normalization.)
2. Differentiate $r$:
   $$
     dr=\frac{1}{2r}\frac{2x^\top dx}{d}=\frac{x^\top dx}{d\,r}.
   $$
   (The quadratic differential is $2x^\top dx$.)
3. Differentiate $y=x/r$:
   $$
     dy=\frac{dx}{r}-\frac{x}{r^2}dr
     =\left(\frac{I}{r}-\frac{xx^\top}{d\,r^3}\right)dx.
   $$
   (The quotient rule separates the direct vector path from the scale path.)
4. Since $y=x/r$, $xx^\top/r^2=yy^\top$. Thus
   $$
   \frac{\partial y}{\partial x}=\frac1r\left(I-\frac1d yy^\top\right).
   $$
   (The substitution expresses the Jacobian using the normalized output.)

**Check.** `checks/M0/D-M0.1-04.py` — normalized-vector Jacobian `max_abs_err = 1.396e-10`, scalar-loss gradient `max_abs_err = 3.356e-10`, both `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** RMSNorm removes the radial scale but keeps the mean component. This makes it a different projection from LayerNorm even when both use the same epsilon convention.

### D-M0.1-05 · Reverse-mode chain rule on a four-node graph

**Claim.** For a scalar loss and graph nodes $c$ with children $v$, reverse mode accumulates

$$
\nabla_v L=\sum_{c\in\operatorname{children}(v)}
\left(\frac{\partial c}{\partial v}\right)^\top\nabla_c L.
$$

Use the scalar graph

$$
u=ab,\qquad v=\tanh(u+c),\qquad w=v^2,\qquad L=w+\sin(v),
$$

where $a,b,c,u,v,w,L\in\mathbb{R}$.

**Steps.**

1. The loss has two children paths from $v$: $w=v^2$ and $\sin(v)$. (The graph must sum both downstream contributions.)
2. The reverse seed is $\bar{L}=\partial L/\partial L=1$. (A scalar loss receives unit upstream sensitivity.)
3. Since $L=w+\sin(v)$, $\bar{w}=\partial L/\partial w=1$. (The coefficient of $w$ is one.)
4. Accumulate at $v$:
   $$
   \bar{v}=\left(\frac{\partial w}{\partial v}\right)^\top\bar{w}
   +\frac{\partial\sin(v)}{\partial v}\bar{L}
   =2v+\cos(v).
   $$
   (The sum is the reverse-mode rule at a merging node.)
5. Differentiate $v=\tanh(u+c)$: $\partial v/\partial u=1-v^2$ and $\partial v/\partial c=1-v^2$. (The derivative of tanh at its pre-activation is $1-\tanh^2$.)
6. Propagate to $u$ and $c$:
   $$
   \bar{u}=\bar{v}(1-v^2),\qquad \bar{c}=\bar{v}(1-v^2).
   $$
   (Both edges leave $v$ with the same local derivative.)
7. Differentiate $u=ab$: $\partial u/\partial a=b$ and $\partial u/\partial b=a$. (The product rule gives the two local edge Jacobians.)
8. Propagate to the leaves:
   $$
   \bar{a}=\bar{u}b,\qquad \bar{b}=\bar{u}a.
   $$
   (Each leaf receives the upstream adjoint multiplied by its local derivative.)

**Check.** `checks/M0/D-M0.1-05.py` — all three leaf gradients pass; `max_abs_err = 2.848e-11`, `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** Reverse mode does not choose one path through a graph. It sends an adjoint backward along every edge and adds the contributions when paths merge.

## 6. Closing

**Counterintuitive question.** If a Jacobian is symmetric, can reverse mode skip the transpose? **Worked answer:** the transpose remains part of the chain-rule statement even when symmetry makes its numerical matrix equal to the original. D-M0.1-01 happens to have a symmetric softmax Jacobian, but D-M0.1-05 contains general local Jacobians and requires the transpose to match the declared $(|y|,|x|)$ convention. The safe rule is to write the transpose every time and simplify only after proving symmetry.
