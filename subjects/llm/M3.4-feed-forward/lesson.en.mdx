## 1. Motivation

Attention moves information between positions; the feed-forward network transforms the information at each position. Its activation is therefore not a decorative detail: it controls which local directions receive a smooth gradient, and gated variants spend parameters on a learned product of two branches.

This lesson derives the exact GELU derivative, the derivative of Swish used by SwiGLU, the complete scalar-loss gradients for a SwiGLU block, and the hidden-width calculation that matches a standard $d\to4d\to d$ FFN's parameter count.

## 2. Forward definition

For a scalar $x\in\mathbb{R}$, let $\Phi$ and $\phi$ be the standard normal CDF and PDF:

$$
\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\operatorname{erf}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt2}\right)\right),
\qquad
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}.
$$

GELU is

$$
\operatorname{GELU}(x)=x\Phi(x).
$$

For a model vector $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and hidden width $m$, a standard two-matrix FFN uses

$$
\operatorname{FFN}(x)=\operatorname{GELU}(xW_1)W_2,
\qquad
W_1\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times 4d},\quad
W_2\in\mathbb{R}^{4d\times d}.
$$

The GLU family uses two input branches. Define

$$
\operatorname{Swish}(u)=u\sigma(u),
\qquad
\sigma(u)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-u}},
$$

and the SwiGLU block

$$
\operatorname{SwiGLU}(x)
=\left(\operatorname{Swish}(xW_1)\odot xW_3\right)W_2,
$$

with

$$
W_1,W_3\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m},
\qquad
W_2\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d},
\qquad
\operatorname{SwiGLU}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
$$

All vectors here are row-vector code conventions for $xW$; the mathematical parameter shapes are displayed explicitly.

**Sources.** GELU is introduced by Hendrycks and Gimpel (https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.08415). The GLU variants and the SwiGLU parameterization follow Shazeer (https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05202).

## 3. Derivations

### D-M3.4-01 · GELU, Swish, SwiGLU gradients, and the $8d/3$ width

**Theorem.** The exact GELU derivative is

$$
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dx}\operatorname{GELU}(x)
=\Phi(x)+x\phi(x)
}.
$$

Swish has derivative

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Swish}'(u)
=\sigma(u)+u\sigma(u)(1-\sigma(u))
}.
$$

For a scalar loss $L=\langle g,y\rangle$ with

$$
a=xW_1\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\qquad
b=xW_3\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\qquad
c=\operatorname{Swish}(a)\odot b\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\qquad
y=cW_2\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
$$

the gradients are

$$
\delta_c=gW_2^\top,\qquad
\delta_a=\delta_c\odot b\odot\operatorname{Swish}'(a),\qquad
\delta_b=\delta_c\odot\operatorname{Swish}(a),
$$

$$
\nabla_xL=\delta_aW_1^\top+\delta_bW_3^\top,
\qquad
\nabla_{W_1}L=x^\top\delta_a,
\qquad
\nabla_{W_3}L=x^\top\delta_b,
\qquad
\nabla_{W_2}L=c^\top g.
$$

Finally, matching the standard FFN parameter count gives

$$
2dm+md=3dm
\stackrel{!}{=}8d^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{m=\frac{8d}{3}}.
$$

**Steps.**

1. Apply the product rule to GELU:
   $$
   \frac{d}{dx}[x\Phi(x)]
   =1\cdot\Phi(x)+x\frac{d}{dx}\Phi(x).
   $$
   The derivative of the normal CDF is its density, $\Phi'(x)=\phi(x)$, so
   $$
   \frac{d}{dx}\operatorname{GELU}(x)=\Phi(x)+x\phi(x).
   $$
2. Differentiate the sigmoid:
   $$
   \sigma'(u)
   =\frac{e^{-u}}{(1+e^{-u})^2}
   =\sigma(u)(1-\sigma(u)).
   $$
   Since $\operatorname{Swish}(u)=u\sigma(u)$, the product rule gives
   $$
   \operatorname{Swish}'(u)
   =\sigma(u)+u\sigma'(u)
   =\sigma(u)+u\sigma(u)(1-\sigma(u)).
   $$
3. Write the SwiGLU forward graph with shapes:
   $$
   x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad
   W_1,W_3\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
   \Longrightarrow
   a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{m},
   $$
   $$
   \operatorname{Swish}(a)\odot b=c\in\mathbb{R}^{m},
   \quad
   W_2\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
   \Longrightarrow y=cW_2\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
   $$
4. For $L=\langle g,y\rangle=g y^\top$, the differential through $y=cW_2$ is
   $$
   dL=g(d c\,W_2+c\,dW_2)^\top
   =\langle gW_2^\top,dc\rangle
   +\langle c^\top g,dW_2\rangle_F.
   $$
   Hence $\delta_c=gW_2^\top$ and $\nabla_{W_2}L=c^\top g$.
5. Since $c=\operatorname{Swish}(a)\odot b$, its differential is
   $$
   dc=\operatorname{diag}(b)\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{Swish}'(a))\,da
   +\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{Swish}(a))\,db.
   $$
   Applying the transpose-Jacobian chain rule gives
   $$
   \delta_a=\delta_c\odot b\odot\operatorname{Swish}'(a),
   \qquad
   \delta_b=\delta_c\odot\operatorname{Swish}(a).
   $$
6. The two affine branches have
   $$
   da=dx\,W_1+x\,dW_1,\qquad
   db=dx\,W_3+x\,dW_3.
   $$
   Substituting $\delta_a,\delta_b$ and collecting coefficients of $dx,dW_1,dW_3$ gives
   $$
   \nabla_xL=\delta_aW_1^\top+\delta_bW_3^\top,
   $$
   $$
   \nabla_{W_1}L=x^\top\delta_a,\qquad
   \nabla_{W_3}L=x^\top\delta_b.
   $$
7. The standard FFN has two matrices with $4d^2$ parameters each, so $8d^2$ total, ignoring biases. SwiGLU has two input matrices with $dm$ parameters each and one output matrix with $md$ parameters:
   $$
   dm+dm+md=3dm.
   $$
   Equating $3dm=8d^2$ and dividing by $3d$ gives $m=8d/3$. For $d=12$, the exact matching width is $m=32$, and both designs contain $1152$ weights.
8. The numerical check verifies the GELU Jacobian, all four SwiGLU parameter gradients, and the parameter-count equality. It reports GELU Jacobian maximum absolute error $3.256264147211141\times10^{-10}$ and SwiGLU gradient maximum absolute error $3.388396258019455\times10^{-9}$.

**Check.** checks/M3/D-M3.4-01.py uses the shared float64 harness for GELU and the complete SwiGLU scalar-loss gradient, then checks $m=8d/3$ at $d=12$; it prints passed = True.

**Claim, correctly scoped.** Equal parameter count does not imply equal optimization dynamics or equal quality. The equation $m=8d/3$ only matches the number of dense weights under the stated two-branch parameterization and ignores biases, normalization, and implementation rounding.

**Approximation, labeled.** Production implementations sometimes replace exact GELU with a tanh-based approximation. This lesson's executable check uses the exact $\operatorname{erf}$ definition above, so no approximation error is folded into the derivative verification.

**Intuition.** GELU gates a coordinate by its Gaussian-CDF probability. SwiGLU lets one learned branch modulate another, so it spends a third matrix on multiplicative selectivity and compensates by using a narrower hidden layer.

## 6. Closing

**Counterintuitive question.** Why can a SwiGLU FFN use a hidden width of only $8d/3$ and still have the same dense parameter count as a $4d$ FFN? **Worked answer:** the standard FFN has two matrices, each contributing $4d^2$, for $8d^2$ total. SwiGLU has three matrices, with two of shape $d\times m$ and one of shape $m\times d$, for $3dm$ total. Setting $3dm=8d^2$ gives $m=8d/3$; the smaller hidden width compensates for the extra input branch.
