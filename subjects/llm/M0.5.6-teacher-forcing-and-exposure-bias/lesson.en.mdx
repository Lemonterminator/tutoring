## 1. Motivation

Teacher forcing supplies the observed prefix when a sequence model predicts the next token. At inference, the model must supply its own prefix. The local conditional model can therefore be evaluated under one prefix distribution and deployed under another. This lesson writes that distribution shift explicitly and derives a simple compounding-error bound. The bound is a labeled **approximation**: it isolates exposure bias with a union bound and does not claim to describe every training or decoding effect.

## 2. Forward definition

Let $x_{1:T}$ be a sequence, let $p_{mathrm{data}}$ be the data distribution, and let $p_	heta$ be the model distribution. At time $t$, define a scalar token cost

$$
\ell_	heta(x_t\mid x_{<t})=-\log p_	heta(x_t\mid x_{<t})\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}.
$$

Teacher forcing evaluates this cost on prefixes drawn from data:

$$
J_{\mathrm{TF}}(\theta)=
\mathbb{E}_{x_{1:T}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}
\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})\right].
$$

Inference instead rolls out prefixes from $p_\theta$. Writing $q_t^{\mathrm{data}}$ and $q_t^\theta$ for the distributions of $x_{<t}$ under those two processes,

$$
J_{\mathrm{roll}}(\theta)=
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{x_{<t}\sim q_t^\theta}
\mathbb{E}_{x_t\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot\mid x_{<t})}
\left[\ell_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})\right].
$$

The conditional target may stay the same while the prefix measure changes. That is the source of exposure bias in this lesson.

## 3. Derivations

### D-M0.5.6-01 · The evaluation distribution changes at inference

**Claim.** The difference between inference-time and teacher-forced token costs is the sum of the same cost function integrated against two different prefix distributions.

**Steps.**

1. Define the expected data-prefix cost at time $t$:
   $$
   J_t^{\mathrm{data}}=
   \mathbb{E}_{x_{<t}\sim q_t^{\mathrm{data}}}
   \mathbb{E}_{x_t\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot\mid x_{<t})}
   [\ell_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})].
   $$
   (Teacher forcing replaces the preceding generated tokens by the observed prefix.)
2. Define the expected model-prefix cost at time $t$:
   $$
   J_t^\theta=
   \mathbb{E}_{x_{<t}\sim q_t^\theta}
   \mathbb{E}_{x_t\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}(\cdot\mid x_{<t})}
   [\ell_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})].
   $$
   (At inference the prefix is generated by the model; the next-token target is still written under the data conditional for this comparison.)
3. Sum over time:
   $$
   J_{\mathrm{TF}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}J_t^{\mathrm{data}},
   \qquad
   J_{\mathrm{roll}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}J_t^\theta.
   $$
   (The sequence cost is additive across prediction times.)
4. Subtract the two expressions:
   $$
   J_{\mathrm{roll}}-J_{\mathrm{TF}}
   =\sum_{t=1}^{T}(J_t^\theta-J_t^{\mathrm{data}}).
   $$
   (Only the prefix measure differs.)
5. For the checked three-prefix fixture, use
   $q_t^{\mathrm{data}}=(0.55,0.30,0.15)$,
   $q_t^\theta=(0.20,0.35,0.45)$, and costs $(0.1,0.7,1.2)$. Then
   $$
   J_t^{\mathrm{data}}=0.55(0.1)+0.30(0.7)+0.15(1.2)=0.40,
   $$
   $$
   J_t^\theta=0.20(0.1)+0.35(0.7)+0.45(1.2)=0.785,
   $$
   $$
   J_t^\theta-J_t^{\mathrm{data}}=0.385.
   $$
   (The fixture makes the distribution shift observable without hiding it in a simulator.)

**Check.** `checks/M0.5/D-M0.5.6-01.py` reports `training_expectation = 0.4`, `inference_expectation = 0.785`, and `shift_identity_error = 0.0`; `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** A low teacher-forced loss answers “how well does the model score data prefixes?” Inference asks “how well does it score prefixes that it itself creates?” These are different expectations even when the token-level formula is identical.

### D-M0.5.6-02 · A simplified compounding-error bound

**Approximation.** Suppose the probability of making a wrong prefix decision at any one step, conditional on having stayed on the data manifold so far, is at most $\epsilon\in[0,1]$. A union bound gives an exposure probability at time $t$ of at most $(t-1)\epsilon$. This is a deliberately simplified upper bound, not a theorem about all trained models.

**Steps.**

1. Let $E_s$ be the event that the generated prefix first deviates from the data prefix at step $s$. For a prefix of length $t-1$, the bad event is
   $$
   E_{<t}=\bigcup_{s=1}^{t-1}E_s.
   $$
   (A prefix is off-manifold if at least one previous decision was wrong.)
2. Apply the union bound:
   $$
   \Pr(E_{<t})\le\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\Pr(E_s)\le\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\epsilon=(t-1)\epsilon.
   $$
   (The union bound does not require the error events to be independent.)
3. Clip the probability at one:
   $$
   \Pr(E_{<t})\le\min\{1,(t-1)\epsilon\}.
   $$
   (Probabilities cannot exceed one.)
4. Summing the un-clipped bound over a horizon $T$ gives the simplified sequence-level exposure term:
   $$
   \sum_{t=1}^{T}(t-1)\epsilon
   =\epsilon\sum_{r=0}^{T-1}r
   =\epsilon\frac{T(T-1)}{2}
   =O(T^2\epsilon).
   $$
   (The arithmetic series counts how many earlier opportunities can contaminate each later prefix.)
5. A teacher-forced per-step error bound summed over the same horizon is
   $$
   \sum_{t=1}^{T}\epsilon=T\epsilon=O(T\epsilon).
   $$
   (Every teacher-forced step is evaluated on a data prefix, so this simplified comparison has no growing prefix-risk factor.)
6. For the checked fixture $T=10$ and $\epsilon=0.01$,
   $$
   T\epsilon=0.1,
   \qquad
   \epsilon\frac{T(T-1)}2=0.45,
   \qquad
   \frac{0.45}{0.1}=4.5.
   $$
   (The ratio is $\frac{T-1}{2}$ for this unclipped approximation.)

**Check.** `checks/M0.5/D-M0.5.6-02.py` reports teacher-forced bound `0.1`, exposure bound `0.45`, union-bound sum `0.45`, and ratio `4.5`; `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** One local error creates a prefix that can affect many later decisions. The quadratic term is a warning about sequence length, not a prediction that every model's measured loss must grow exactly quadratically.

## 6. Closing

**Counterintuitive question.** If teacher forcing uses the correct observed prefix, why can its loss be an unreliable deployment predictor? **Worked answer:** D-M0.5.6-01 shows that training and rollout integrate the same local cost against different prefix distributions. D-M0.5.6-02 shows, under a labeled simplifying assumption, why prefix errors can accumulate with sequence length.

**Assignment A-M0.5.** Extend the common assignment implementation so it reports teacher-forced NLL and a rollout estimate side by side, then compare both with the V-M0.5-e curves. Keep the data/model prefix distributions explicit in the report.

**Spine check.** Architecture-wise, an RNN still rolls hidden state forward sequentially, while a causal Transformer can train all teacher-forced positions in parallel because future tokens are masked. Probability-wise, both still distinguish the data-prefix measure used for training from the model-prefix measure used during generation.

**Reference.** Ross and Bagnell, “A Reduction of Imitation Learning and Structured Prediction to No-Regret Online Learning,” [PMLR 15 (2011)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html). The reference motivates the distribution-shift perspective; the bound above is the lesson's explicitly labeled simplified calculation.
