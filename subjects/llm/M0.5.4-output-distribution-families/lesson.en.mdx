## 1. Motivation

M0.5.3 made a conditional probability explicit as a count table. The next defect is treating “the output distribution” as a cosmetic choice. Bernoulli and categorical heads produce discrete likelihoods, a fixed-variance Gaussian head produces a mean-seeking squared-error objective, and a mixture head can represent multiple plausible outcomes. This lesson derives those losses and the stable numerics needed to compute them.

## 2. Forward definition

For a scalar logit $z\in\mathbb{R}$, define $\sigma(z)=1/(1+e^{-z})\in(0,1)$. For a categorical logit vector $z\in\mathbb{R}^{V}$, let $p=\operatorname{softmax}(z)\in\Delta^{V-1}$. Their negative log-likelihoods for a binary target $y\in\{0,1\}$ and categorical target $y\in\{1,\ldots,V\}$ are

$$
\mathrm{BCE}(z,y)=-y\log\sigma(z)-(1-y)\log(1-\sigma(z))\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0},
\qquad
\mathrm{CE}(z,y)=-\log p_y\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}.
$$

For a scalar target $x\in\mathbb{R}$, a Gaussian head uses mean $\mu\in\mathbb{R}$ and fixed scale $\sigma>0$:

$$
p(x\mid\mu,\sigma)=\mathcal{N}(x;\mu,\sigma^2)\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0},
\qquad
\mathrm{NLL}(x;\mu,\sigma)=\frac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)+\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

For a $K$-component mixture, $x,\mu_k\in\mathbb{R}$, $z_\pi, z_\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$, and $\pi\in\Delta^{K-1}$. In D-M0.5.4-03 we use the sign convention fixed by the specification, $\pi=\operatorname{softmax}(-z_\pi)$, so the requested logit gradient is $\gamma-\pi$:

$$
p(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k\mathcal{N}(x;\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0},
\qquad
\mathrm{NLL}(x)=-\log p(x)\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

## 3. Derivations

### D-M0.5.4-01 · Bernoulli BCE and categorical CE

**Claim.** The Bernoulli logit gradient is $\partial\mathrm{BCE}/\partial z=\sigma(z)-y\in\mathbb{R}$, and the categorical logit gradient is $\nabla_z\mathrm{CE}=p-e_y\in\mathbb{R}^{V}$.

**Steps.**

1. Write the Bernoulli probability as $q=\sigma(z)\in(0,1)$ and the loss as
   $$
   L(q,y)=-y\log q-(1-y)\log(1-q)\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}.
   $$
   (The two terms select the likelihood of the observed binary outcome.)
2. Differentiate with respect to $q$:
   $$
   \frac{\partial L}{\partial q}=-\frac{y}{q}+\frac{1-y}{1-q}\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The derivative of $-\log(1-q)$ contributes the positive reciprocal term.)
3. Differentiate the sigmoid:
   $$
   \frac{\partial q}{\partial z}=q(1-q)\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (Differentiate $q=(1+e^{-z})^{-1}$ and rewrite the result using $q$.)
4. Apply the scalar chain rule and simplify:
   $$
   \frac{\partial L}{\partial z}=\left(-\frac{y}{q}+\frac{1-y}{1-q}\right)q(1-q)=q-y=\sigma(z)-y\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The factors $q$ and $1-q$ cancel the two denominators.)
5. For a categorical target, use the softmax cross-entropy from D-M0.1-02:
   $$
   L(z,y)=-\log\operatorname{softmax}(z)_y\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}.
   $$
   (The categorical head assigns one probability to each of the $V$ outcomes.)
6. Apply D-M0.1-02 directly:
   $$
   \nabla_zL=p-e_y\in\mathbb{R}^{V}.
   $$
   (The prior derivation already includes the softmax Jacobian and the target coordinate.)

**Check.** `checks/M0.5/D-M0.5.4-01.py` — BCE max absolute error `1.218e-10`, CE max absolute error `1.560e-10`, `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** BCE and CE are not arbitrary names for loss functions. They are negative log-likelihoods whose simple logit gradients arise from the corresponding output distributions.

### D-M0.5.4-02 · Fixed-variance Gaussian NLL is MSE up to constants

**Claim.** For fixed $\sigma>0$, minimizing Gaussian NLL over $\mu$ is equivalent to minimizing squared error, and a single Gaussian predicts the conditional mean. On a symmetric bimodal target, that mean lies between the modes.

**Steps.**

1. Start with the scalar Gaussian density and take its negative logarithm:
   $$
   -\log p(x\mid\mu,\sigma)=\frac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)+\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (Taking the negative logarithm converts the density product used in likelihood training into an additive loss.)
2. For fixed $\sigma$, define the constant $C_\sigma=\frac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)\in\mathbb{R}$:
   $$
   \mathrm{NLL}(x;\mu,\sigma)=C_\sigma+\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}(x-\mu)^2\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The first term does not depend on the predicted mean.)
3. Over data $x_1,\ldots,x_N\in\mathbb{R}$, average the loss:
   $$
   \overline{\mathrm{NLL}}(\mu)=C_\sigma+\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_i-\mu)^2\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (Averaging preserves the same additive constant and positive scale.)
4. Differentiate with respect to $\mu$:
   $$
   \frac{d\overline{\mathrm{NLL}}}{d\mu}=\frac{1}{N\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mu-x_i)\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The derivative of $(x_i-\mu)^2$ is $2(\mu-x_i)$.)
5. Set the derivative to zero:
   $$
   0=N\mu-\sum_ix_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\hat\mu=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The unique quadratic minimizer is the sample mean.)
6. For the bimodal target $x\in\{-1,+1\}$ with equal mass, $\hat\mu=0\in\mathbb{R}$. (The mean lies in a low-density gap between the two modes.)
7. Therefore a fixed-variance single Gaussian has the same optimizer as MSE and blurs this bimodal target, while a mixture can put separate mass near both modes. (The output family, not the hidden representation alone, determines this uncertainty behavior.)

**Check.** `checks/M0.5/D-M0.5.4-02.py` — mean `0.0`, MSE `1.0`, NLL/MSE equivalence error `0.0`, `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** Predicting the mean is optimal for a single squared-error bowl. When the target has two valid answers, that bowl places its optimum between them.

### D-M0.5.4-03 · Mixture-density NLL and posterior responsibilities

**Claim.** Let $\gamma_k$ be the posterior responsibility of component $k$. With the D-M0.5.4 specification's sign convention $\pi=\operatorname{softmax}(-z_\pi)$,

$$
\frac{\partial\mathrm{NLL}}{\partial\mu_k}=-\gamma_k\frac{x-\mu_k}{\sigma_k^2},
\qquad
\frac{\partial\mathrm{NLL}}{\partial\log\sigma_k}=-\gamma_k\left(\frac{(x-\mu_k)^2}{\sigma_k^2}-1\right),
\qquad
\nabla_{z_\pi}\mathrm{NLL}=\gamma-\pi\in\mathbb{R}^{K}.
$$

**Steps.**

1. Define the component density and mixture density:
   $$
   q_k=\mathcal{N}(x;\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},
   \qquad
   S=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_jq_j\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},
   \qquad
   \mathrm{NLL}=-\log S\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The positive mixture density is the sum of weighted positive component densities.)
2. Define the posterior responsibility:
   $$
   \gamma_k=\frac{\pi_kq_k}{S}\in[0,1],
   \qquad
   \sum_{k=1}^{K}\gamma_k=1.
   $$
   (Bayes' rule normalizes the component contribution for the observed $x$.)
3. Differentiate the Gaussian log density with respect to its mean:
   $$
   \frac{\partial\log q_k}{\partial\mu_k}=\frac{x-\mu_k}{\sigma_k^2}\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (Only the squared residual depends on $\mu_k$.)
4. Differentiate the mixture NLL through $S$:
   $$
   \frac{\partial\mathrm{NLL}}{\partial\mu_k}=-\frac{1}{S}\pi_k\frac{\partial q_k}{\partial\mu_k}
   =-\frac{\pi_kq_k}{S}\frac{\partial\log q_k}{\partial\mu_k}
   =-\gamma_k\frac{x-\mu_k}{\sigma_k^2}.
   $$
   (The responsibility appears when the component derivative is divided by the full mixture density.)
5. Let $r_k=\log\sigma_k\in\mathbb{R}$, so $\sigma_k=e^{r_k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$. Differentiate:
   $$
   \frac{\partial\log q_k}{\partial r_k}=\frac{(x-\mu_k)^2}{\sigma_k^2}-1\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The log scale contributes one normalization term and one residual-scale term.)
6. Apply the same mixture weighting:
   $$
   \frac{\partial\mathrm{NLL}}{\partial r_k}=-\gamma_k\left(\frac{(x-\mu_k)^2}{\sigma_k^2}-1\right)\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The responsibility weights the component's scale evidence.)
7. Use $\pi=\operatorname{softmax}(-z_\pi)$ and $\partial\log S/\partial\log\pi_k=\gamma_k$:
   $$
   \frac{\partial\mathrm{NLL}}{\partial z_{\pi,k}}=\gamma_k-\pi_k\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The negative sign in the chosen parameterization contributes the requested sign.)
8. Collect the three coordinate families into the displayed gradient. (This is the point where EM's responsibility calculation and gradient descent's parameter update meet.)

**Check.** `checks/M0.5/D-M0.5.4-03.py` — joint parameter gradient max absolute error `1.866e-10`; responsibilities `[0.2381187835, 0.5459244470, 0.2159567700]`; `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** Each observation distributes responsibility across components. A component moves its mean and scale according to its responsibility, while the mixture logits compete through the normalized responsibility vector.

### D-M0.5.4-04 · Stable log-sum-exp and output reparameterizations

**Claim.** For $a\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$, the log-sum-exp identity

$$
\operatorname{lse}(a)=\log\sum_{k=1}^{K}e^{a_k}=m+\log\sum_{k=1}^{K}e^{a_k-m},\qquad m=\max_k a_k\in\mathbb{R}
$$

is exact, and no exponentiated term exceeds $1$. The reparameterizations $\sigma=e^{z_\sigma}$ and $\pi=\operatorname{softmax}(z_\pi)$ have scalar derivative $\partial\sigma/\partial z_\sigma=\sigma$ and Jacobian $\operatorname{diag}(\pi)-\pi\pi^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}$.

**Steps.**

1. Factor out $e^m$ from the positive sum:
   $$
   \sum_ke^{a_k}=\sum_ke^{m}e^{a_k-m}=e^m\sum_ke^{a_k-m}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.
   $$
   (The same scalar factor $e^m$ multiplies every term.)
2. Take logarithms:
   $$
   \log\sum_ke^{a_k}=\log e^m+\log\sum_ke^{a_k-m}=m+\log\sum_ke^{a_k-m}\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The logarithm turns the factored product into a sum.)
3. Since $m=\max_k a_k$, every shifted exponent satisfies $a_k-m\le0$:
   $$
   0<e^{a_k-m}\le1\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (No individual exponential can overflow after the shift.)
4. For the positive scale reparameterization $\sigma=e^{z_\sigma}$:
   $$
   \frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial z_\sigma}=e^{z_\sigma}=\sigma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.
   $$
   (The exponential derivative preserves positivity.)
5. For $\pi=\operatorname{softmax}(z_\pi)\in\Delta^{K-1}$, D-M0.1-01 gives
   $$
   \frac{\partial\pi}{\partial z_\pi}=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)-\pi\pi^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}.
   $$
   (The normalization couples all categorical coordinates.)
6. Therefore stable log-sum-exp is algebraically exact but numerically safer, while the exponential and softmax maps enforce the output constraints. (Stability changes the evaluation path, not the represented real-valued expression.)

**Check.** `checks/M0.5/D-M0.5.4-04.py` — stable log-sum-exp error `0.0`, softmax sum `1.0`, Jacobian symmetry error `0.0`, `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** Subtracting the maximum does not approximate log-sum-exp. It changes the order of operations so that floating-point exponentials stay in a safe range.

### D-M0.5.4-05 · Discretized logistic mixtures over bins

**Claim.** For a logistic component with mean $\mu\in\mathbb{R}$ and log scale $r\in\mathbb{R}$, the probability of a bin $[\ell,u]\subset\mathbb{R}$ is the CDF difference

$$
P(\ell\le X\le u)=\operatorname{sigmoid}\left(\frac{u-\mu}{e^r}\right)-\operatorname{sigmoid}\left(\frac{\ell-\mu}{e^r}\right)\in[0,1].
$$

A mixture sums these bin masses with weights $\pi_k$, so categorical bins and continuous mixture-density outputs are two ends of one likelihood framework.

**Steps.**

1. Define the logistic CDF for scalar $z\in\mathbb{R}$:
   $$
   F(z;\mu,r)=\operatorname{sigmoid}\left(\frac{z-\mu}{e^r}\right)\in(0,1).
   $$
   (The positive scale is $e^r$ and the sigmoid maps the standardized coordinate to a CDF value.)
2. Integrate the density over a bin by evaluating the CDF at its endpoints:
   $$
   P(\ell\le X\le u)=F(u;\mu,r)-F(\ell;\mu,r)\in[0,1].
   $$
   (The fundamental theorem of calculus turns the density integral into a CDF difference.)
3. For $K$ components, weight and add the component bin probabilities:
   $$
   P(\ell\le X\le u)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k\left[F(u;\mu_k,r_k)-F(\ell;\mu_k,r_k)\right]\in[0,1].
   $$
   (Mixture probability is the weighted sum of component probabilities.)
4. If the output bins are the complete discrete alphabet, these bin masses form a categorical distribution. (The continuous density has been converted into finite-bin probabilities.)
5. If the bins are fine and the mixture parameters remain continuous, the same expression approximates a continuous density likelihood. (The bin width controls the discretization rather than changing the mixture principle.)
6. Terminology note: the original NADE by Larochelle and Murray (2011) is a binary-data model with weight-shared hidden units; the mixture-density recurrent model in the lecture is closer to RNADE/MDN-RNN. (These names identify different output constructions and should not be conflated.)

Primary terminology sources: [NADE](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/larochelle11a.html), [RNADE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.0186), and [PixelCNN++](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.05517).

**Check.** `checks/M0.5/D-M0.5.4-05.py` — minimum bin probability is nonnegative; covered mass `0.999...`; `passed = True`.

**Intuition.** A categorical head assigns mass directly to bins. A logistic-mixture head assigns mass by integrating smooth components over the same bins, so the two heads differ in parameterization and resolution rather than in the probability bookkeeping.

## 6. Closing

**Counterintuitive question.** Why can a better hidden representation still produce blurred predictions? **Worked answer:** D-M0.5.4-02 shows that a fixed-variance single Gaussian optimizes MSE and therefore predicts the conditional mean. The output family discards multimodality even if the hidden state contains useful information. D-M0.5.4-03 shows how a mixture restores multiple modes through responsibilities.
