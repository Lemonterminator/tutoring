## 1. Motivation

M0.5 fixed the probability factorization. The remaining defect is architectural: a vanilla RNN stores the prefix in a recursively updated hidden state, so a gradient must cross a product of recurrent Jacobians. This lesson unrolls that graph, derives every shared parameter gradient, and separates three effects that are often conflated: saturation, truncation, and gradient clipping.

## 2. Forward definition

Let $x_t\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$, $h_t\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$, $z_t\in\mathbb{R}^{V}$, and $p_t\in\Delta^{V-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{V}$. Let

$$
W_h\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times H},\quad
W_x\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D},\quad
b\in\mathbb{R}^{H},\quad
W_y\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times H},\quad
c\in\mathbb{R}^{V}.
$$

With $h_0=0\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$, the recurrence and output are

$$
a_t=W_hh_{t-1}+W_xx_t+b\in\mathbb{R}^{H},
\qquad
h_t=\tanh(a_t)\in\mathbb{R}^{H},
$$

$$
z_t=W_yh_t+c\in\mathbb{R}^{V},
\qquad
p_t=\operatorname{softmax}(z_t)\in\Delta^{V-1}.
$$

For next-token teacher forcing, the target $x_{t+1}$ is represented by an index in $\{1,\ldots,V\}$ and $e_{x_{t+1}}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}$ is its one-hot vector. The scalar loss and sequence loss are

$$
\ell_t=-\log p_t[x_{t+1}]\in\mathbb{R},
\qquad
L=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_t\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

The RNN is sequential in $h_t$ even when all teacher-forced inputs are known before the forward pass.

## 3. Derivations

### D-M1.1-01 · Per-step output gradient

**Theorem.** For $\ell_t=-\log p_t[x_{t+1}]$ with $p_t=\operatorname{softmax}(z_t)$,

$$
\frac{\partial\ell_t}{\partial z_t}=p_t-e_{x_{t+1}}\in\mathbb{R}^{V},
\qquad
\nabla_{W_y}\ell_t=(p_t-e_{x_{t+1}})h_t^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times H}.
$$

**Steps.**

1. For $p_i=\exp(z_i)/\sum_j\exp(z_j)$, differentiate coordinate $i$ with respect to logit $z_j$:
   $$
   \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}=p_i(\mathbf{1}_{i=j}-p_j)\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (The quotient rule gives one direct numerator term and one shared-denominator term.)
2. The loss derivative with respect to probability coordinate $p_i$ is
   $$
   \frac{\partial\ell_t}{\partial p_i}
   =-\frac{\mathbf{1}_{i=x_{t+1}}}{p_{x_{t+1}}}\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (Only the target coordinate appears in the negative log.)
3. Apply the scalar chain rule for logit coordinate $z_j$:
   $$
   \frac{\partial\ell_t}{\partial z_j}
   =\sum_i\frac{\partial\ell_t}{\partial p_i}
   \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}
   =-\frac{p_{x_{t+1}}(\mathbf{1}_{x_{t+1}=j}-p_j)}{p_{x_{t+1}}}
   =p_j-\mathbf{1}_{j=x_{t+1}}.
   $$
   (The target probability cancels.)
4. Collect the $V$ scalar coordinates:
   $$
   \nabla_{z_t}\ell_t=p_t-e_{x_{t+1}}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}.
   $$
   (This is the same softmax-cross-entropy result checked in D-M0.1-02.)
5. For $z_t=W_yh_t+c$, a perturbation of $W_y$ gives
   $$
   dz_t=(dW_y)h_t\in\mathbb{R}^{V}.
   $$
   The scalar differential is
   $$
   d\ell_t=(p_t-e_{x_{t+1}})^\top(dW_y)h_t
   =\operatorname{tr}\left(\left[(p_t-e_{x_{t+1}})h_t^\top\right]^\top dW_y\right).
   $$
   (The trace form identifies the matrix gradient under the Frobenius inner product.)
6. Therefore
   $$
   \nabla_{W_y}\ell_t=(p_t-e_{x_{t+1}})h_t^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times H}.
   $$
   (Each output row receives the scalar logit error multiplied by the hidden activation.)

**Check.** checks/M1/D-M1.1-01.py compares the logit gradient with float64 central differences and checks the outer-product matrix form; passed = True.

**Intuition.** The output layer sees an error vector in vocabulary space. The matrix gradient maps that vector back onto the hidden activation that caused the prediction.

### D-M1.1-02 · Backward recursion in the hidden state

**Theorem.** Define $\delta_t=\nabla_{h_t}L\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$. With $\delta_{T+1}=0\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$,

$$
\delta_t
=W_y^\top(p_t-e_{x_{t+1}})
+W_h^\top\left(\delta_{t+1}\odot(1-h_{t+1}^{\odot2})\right)
\in\mathbb{R}^{H}.
$$

The first term is the direct output path at time $t$; the second is the indirect path through $h_{t+1}$.

In one line, the recursion is $\delta_t = W_y^\top(p_t-e_{x_{t+1}})+W_h^\top(\delta_{t+1}\odot(1-h_{t+1}^{\odot2}))$ with every term in $\mathbb{R}^{H}$.

**Steps.**

1. The direct path through $z_t=W_yh_t+c$ contributes
   $$
   \left.\nabla_{h_t}\ell_t\right|_{\mathrm{direct}}
   =W_y^\top\nabla_{z_t}\ell_t
   =W_y^\top(p_t-e_{x_{t+1}})\in\mathbb{R}^{H}.
   $$
   (Transpose the linear map from hidden space to vocabulary space.)
2. The next hidden state is
   $$
   h_{t+1}=\tanh(W_hh_t+W_xx_{t+1}+b)\in\mathbb{R}^{H}.
   $$
   Its local Jacobian is
   $$
   \frac{\partial h_{t+1}}{\partial h_t}
   =\operatorname{diag}(1-h_{t+1}^{\odot2})W_h\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times H}.
   $$
   (The derivative of tanh is applied coordinatewise.)
3. The indirect path applies the transpose Jacobian to the upstream state gradient:
   $$
   \left.\nabla_{h_t}L\right|_{\mathrm{indirect}}
   =\left(\frac{\partial h_{t+1}}{\partial h_t}\right)^\top\delta_{t+1}
   =W_h^\top\left(\delta_{t+1}\odot(1-h_{t+1}^{\odot2})\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{H}.
   $$
   (Reverse mode transposes the forward Jacobian.)
4. Add the two paths:
   $$
   \delta_t
   =\left.\nabla_{h_t}\ell_t\right|_{\mathrm{direct}}
   +\left.\nabla_{h_t}L\right|_{\mathrm{indirect}}.
   $$
   (The total loss contains the local output and all later outputs.)
5. At the final prediction there is no later recurrent state, so $\delta_{T+1}=0\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$. This makes the recursion terminate at $t=T$.
6. For the numerical fixture, independently perturb $h_0\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$, rerun the forward loss, and compare the finite-difference gradient with the recursively propagated $\nabla_{h_0}L$. (The check tests the complete chain, not only a symbolic substitution.)

**Check.** checks/M1/D-M1.1-02.py reports a finite-difference maximum absolute error below 8e-10 and a recursion identity error of 0.0; passed = True.

**Intuition.** Every time step contributes a direct error, and every later time step sends an additional error backward through the recurrent edge. BPTT is the sum of these paths.

### D-M1.1-03 · Parameter gradients and weight sharing

**Theorem.** Let

$$
\eta_t=\delta_t\odot(1-h_t^{\odot2})\in\mathbb{R}^{H}
$$

be the pre-activation gradient. Then

$$
\nabla_{W_h}L=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_t h_{t-1}^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times H},
\qquad
\nabla_{W_x}L=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_t x_t^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D},
$$

$$
\nabla_bL=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_t\in\mathbb{R}^{H},
\qquad
\nabla_{W_y}L=\sum_{t=1}^{T}(p_t-e_{x_{t+1}})h_t^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times H},
\qquad
\nabla_cL=\sum_{t=1}^{T}(p_t-e_{x_{t+1}})\in\mathbb{R}^{V}.
$$

**Steps.**

1. From $h_t=\tanh(a_t)$, the coordinatewise derivative gives
   $$
   da_t=\eta_t\in\mathbb{R}^{H},
   \qquad
   \eta_t=\delta_t\odot(1-h_t^{\odot2}).
   $$
   (This is the local reverse-mode derivative through tanh.)
2. For $a_t=W_hh_{t-1}+W_xx_t+b$, perturbing $W_h$ gives $da_t=(dW_h)h_{t-1}$. Thus
   $$
   dL=\eta_t^\top(dW_h)h_{t-1}
   =\operatorname{tr}\left((\eta_t h_{t-1}^\top)^\top dW_h\right),
   $$
   so the time-$t$ contribution is $\eta_t h_{t-1}^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times H}$.
3. Perturbing $W_x$ gives $da_t=(dW_x)x_t$, hence the time-$t$ contribution is
   $$
   \eta_t x_t^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D}.
   $$
   (The input and hidden vectors occupy different right-hand spaces.)
4. Perturbing $b$ gives $da_t=db$, so the time-$t$ contribution is
   $$
   \nabla_b\ell_t=\eta_t\in\mathbb{R}^{H}.
   $$
   (A shared bias receives one vector contribution per time.)
5. Add the contributions across time because the same $W_h$, $W_x$, and $b$ are reused:
   $$
   \nabla_{W_h}L=\sum_t\eta_th_{t-1}^\top,\qquad
   \nabla_{W_x}L=\sum_t\eta_tx_t^\top,\qquad
   \nabla_bL=\sum_t\eta_t.
   $$
   (This sum is the mathematical signature of weight sharing.)
6. D-M1.1-01 gives the output-layer contributions, and summing them over time yields $\nabla_{W_y}L$ and $\nabla_cL$. (The output parameters are shared in exactly the same additive way.)
7. The check uses $H=8$, $D=5$, $V=5$, and $T=6$, then compares all five parameter arrays with float64 finite differences. (Every reported shape is checked by the harness.)

**Check.** checks/M1/D-M1.1-03.py checks $W_h$, $W_x$, $b$, $W_y$, and $c$ at rtol = 1e-5; maximum absolute error is 1.786e-09; passed = True.

**Intuition.** A single parameter participates in every unrolled copy of the cell. Backpropagation adds the gradient from all copies instead of creating separate parameters for each time.

### D-M1.1-04 · The unrolled Jacobian product

**Theorem.** For a lag $k\ge1$, the Jacobian from $h_{t-k}\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$ to $h_t\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$ is

$$
\frac{\partial h_t}{\partial h_{t-k}}
=J_tJ_{t-1}\cdots J_{t-k+1}
=\prod_{s=t-k+1}^{t}{}^{\leftarrow}
\left[\operatorname{diag}(1-h_s^{\odot2})W_h\right]
\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times H},
$$

where $J_s=\operatorname{diag}(1-h_s^{\odot2})W_h$ and the left arrow states that larger time indices multiply on the left. The product order is not interchangeable for matrices.

**Steps.**

1. For one recurrent edge,
   $$
   \frac{\partial h_s}{\partial h_{s-1}}
   =J_s=\operatorname{diag}(1-h_s^{\odot2})W_h\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times H}.
   $$
   (This is the local Jacobian already used in D-M1.1-02.)
2. For $k=2$, the multivariable chain rule gives
   $$
   \frac{\partial h_t}{\partial h_{t-2}}
   =\frac{\partial h_t}{\partial h_{t-1}}
    \frac{\partial h_{t-1}}{\partial h_{t-2}}
   =J_tJ_{t-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times H}.
   $$
   (The later map is applied after the earlier map, so it is the left factor.)
3. Repeating the chain rule for $k$ edges gives
   $$
   \frac{\partial h_t}{\partial h_{t-k}}
   =J_tJ_{t-1}\cdots J_{t-k+1}.
   $$
   (Each extra edge contributes one Jacobian on the left.)
4. The numerical check fixes the inputs and varies only $h_{t-k}\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$. It compares the product above with the complete vector Jacobian computed by central differences. (The test uses $H=3$, $k=4$, and smooth tanh states.)

**Check.** checks/M1/D-M1.1-04.py reports maximum Jacobian absolute error 1.345e-11; passed = True.

**Intuition.** A long-range gradient is not one number. It is a matrix product whose factors depend on both the recurrent weights and the saturated hidden states.

### D-M1.1-05 · A norm bound and the one-dimensional case

**Theorem.** For the spectral norm,

$$
\left\|\frac{\partial h_t}{\partial h_{t-k}}\right\|_2
\le\prod_{s=t-k+1}^{t}\left\|\operatorname{diag}(1-h_s^{\odot2})W_h\right\|_2
\le\left(\gamma\,\sigma_{\max}(W_h)\right)^k,
\qquad
\gamma=\sup_u|\tanh'(u)|=1.
$$

Therefore $\sigma_{\max}(W_h)<1$ is sufficient for vanishing in this upper bound. $\sigma_{\max}(W_h)>1$ is necessary, not sufficient, for exploding gradients: saturation can still contract factors.

**Steps.**

1. Apply submultiplicativity to the product from D-M1.1-04:
   $$
   \left\|J_tJ_{t-1}\cdots J_{t-k+1}\right\|_2
   \le\prod_{s=t-k+1}^{t}\|J_s\|_2.
   $$
   (The induced spectral norm bounds a product by the product of norms.)
2. Since $|\tanh'(u)|=1-\tanh^2(u)\le1$, the diagonal derivative matrix obeys
   $$
   \left\|\operatorname{diag}(1-h_s^{\odot2})\right\|_2\le1=\gamma.
   $$
   (For a diagonal matrix, the spectral norm is the largest absolute diagonal entry.)
3. Apply the matrix norm product inequality:
   $$
   \|J_s\|_2
   \le\left\|\operatorname{diag}(1-h_s^{\odot2})\right\|_2\|W_h\|_2
   \le\gamma\sigma_{\max}(W_h).
   $$
   (The spectral norm of $W_h$ is its largest singular value.)
4. Multiply the $k$ identical upper bounds:
   $$
   \left\|\frac{\partial h_t}{\partial h_{t-k}}\right\|_2
   \le(\gamma\sigma_{\max}(W_h))^k.
   $$
   (This is an upper bound, not an equality for a nonlinear saturated trajectory.)
5. If $\sigma_{\max}(W_h)<1$ and $\gamma=1$, the right side decays geometrically with $k$. (This gives a sufficient condition for the bound to vanish.)
6. If a product explodes asymptotically, the upper-bound base cannot be below one, so $\sigma_{\max}(W_h)>1$ is necessary for this mechanism. It is not sufficient because diagonal tanh derivatives can be much smaller than one. (A norm bound cannot certify growth from above.)
7. In one dimension, $h_t=\tanh(wh_{t-1})$ has the exact derivative
   $$
   \frac{\partial h_t}{\partial h_{t-k}}
   =\prod_{s=t-k+1}^{t}w(1-h_s^2).
   $$
   If the recurrence is linear, $h_t=\phi h_{t-1}$, the factors become $\phi$ and the result is $\phi^k$, matching D-M0.5.2-02. (The nonlinear case adds the saturation factors.)
8. The check samples 100 random $W_h$ matrices and 2,100 products, computes each singular norm, and verifies the bound numerically. (The random seed is fixed and all arrays are float64.)

**Check.** checks/M1/D-M1.1-05.py reports products_checked = 2100, worst bound ratio 0.9944786301903756, and passed = True.

**Source note.** The norm-bound framing follows Pascanu, Mikolov, and Bengio, “On the difficulty of training recurrent neural networks,” [PMLR 28 (2013)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/pascanu13.html).

**Intuition.** Orthogonal or unit-spectral-radius weights remove one source of contraction, but tanh still multiplies the path by derivatives below one. The additive memory path introduced in M2 addresses that defect.

### D-M1.1-06 · Truncated BPTT and global-norm clipping

**Approximation.** Truncated BPTT is a biased gradient estimator because it drops recurrent paths older than a chosen window. Global-norm clipping is a direction-preserving update transformation that bounds the gradient norm; it does not repair the dropped paths.

**Steps.**

1. The full hidden gradient expands the recursion:
   $$
   \delta_t
   =\sum_{j=t}^{T}
   \left(\frac{\partial h_j}{\partial h_t}\right)^\top
   W_y^\top(p_j-e_{x_{j+1}})
   \in\mathbb{R}^{H}.
   $$
   (Each later output sends a path back to $h_t$.)
2. With a recurrent window $K\in\mathbb{N}_0$, truncated BPTT keeps only terms with $j-t\le K$:
   $$
   \delta_t^{(K)}
   =\sum_{j=t}^{\min(T,t+K)}
   \left(\frac{\partial h_j}{\partial h_t}\right)^\top
   W_y^\top(p_j-e_{x_{j+1}})\in\mathbb{R}^{H}.
   $$
   (Terms from outputs more than $K$ transitions away are set to zero.)
3. The truncated parameter gradient is therefore
   $$
   g^{(K)}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\text{local contribution from }\delta_t^{(K)},
   $$
   where $g^{(K)}$ has the same parameter shapes as the full gradient. (The omission depends on the data and current parameters, so it is generally biased.)
4. Let the concatenated full gradient be $g\in\mathbb{R}^{P}$ and choose a threshold $c>0$. Define
   $$
   \widetilde g=g\min\left(1,\frac{c}{\|g\|_2}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{P}.
   $$
   (The scalar factor is one below the threshold and scales down the whole vector above it.)
5. If $\|g\|_2>c$, then
   $$
   \|\widetilde g\|_2
   =\|g\|_2\frac{c}{\|g\|_2}=c.
   $$
   If $\|g\|_2\le c$, then $\widetilde g=g$. (Thus the norm is bounded by $c$.)
6. The scalar multiplier is positive, so for $g\ne0$,
   $$
   \frac{\widetilde g}{\|\widetilde g\|_2}
   =\frac{g}{\|g\|_2}.
   $$
   (Clipping preserves direction and changes only magnitude.)
7. The check compares full and $K=2$ gradients, then verifies the clipped norm and cosine direction. (A nonzero full-versus-truncated difference is the numerical signature of bias.)

**Check.** checks/M1/D-M1.1-06.py reports full-versus-truncated difference 0.020061110848026618, clipped norm 0.5, and cosine 1.0000000000000002; passed = True.

**Intuition.** Truncation forgets old causes; clipping limits the size of whatever gradient remains. They solve different numerical problems and should not be described as the same operation.

### D-M1.1-07 · Teacher forcing in the RNN

**Claim.** The RNN recursion above is a teacher-forced training graph: $x_t$ and the target $x_{t+1}$ are supplied by the data sequence. At inference, the next input is sampled from the model's previous output, so the prefix distribution changes as in D-M0.5.6-01.

**Steps.**

1. In the training graph, the known input sequence is
   $$
   (x_1,\ldots,x_T)\in(\mathbb{R}^{D})^T,
   $$
   and each $h_t$ uses the observed $x_t$. (The forward pass does not feed its own prediction back into the next input.)
2. The loss target at time $t$ is the observed next token:
   $$
   \ell_t=-\log p_\theta(x_{t+1}\mid x_{\le t})\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   (This is the RNN form of the autoregressive conditional loss.)
3. In a rollout, generate $\widehat{x}_{t+1}$ from $p_\theta(\cdot\mid\widehat{x}_{\le t})$ and feed that generated vector into the next recurrence:
   $$
   \widehat{x}_{t+1}\sim p_\theta(\cdot\mid\widehat{x}_{\le t}),
   \qquad
   h_{t+1}=F_\theta(h_t,\widehat{x}_{t+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{H}.
   $$
   (The model now supplies the prefix that it must learn to handle.)
4. Therefore training averages costs under data prefixes, while rollout averages them under model prefixes:
   $$
   \mathbb{E}_{x_{<t}\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}[\ell_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})]
   \quad\text{versus}\quad
   \mathbb{E}_{x_{<t}\sim p_\theta}[\ell_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})].
   $$
   (The local recurrent computation is unchanged; the measure over prefixes is not.)
5. Teacher forcing makes all target conditionals available in one training example, but it does not make the hidden recurrence parallel: $h_t$ still depends on $h_{t-1}$. (The causal Transformer will obtain a different parallelism pattern from masked matrix operations in M3.)
6. The check runs the same initial input through teacher forcing and greedy rollout, then verifies that later prefixes differ while the teacher-forced loss remains a finite scalar. (This is a concrete finite-horizon instance of exposure bias.)

**Check.** checks/M1/D-M1.1-07.py reports teacher-forced loss 7.011017626955435, a nonzero later-prefix difference 6.0, and finite hidden-gradient shapes; passed = True.

**Intuition.** Teacher forcing gives BPTT a clean supervised path through the observed sequence. Deployment removes that support, so the cell must operate on its own history.

## 6. Closing

**Counterintuitive question.** Why does orthogonal initialization of $W_h$ with spectral radius exactly one not solve the long-range-gradient problem? **Worked answer:** D-M1.1-05 shows that the recurrent product also contains $\operatorname{diag}(1-h_t^{\odot2})$. Tanh derivatives are below one away from zero, so the product can contract even when $\sigma_{\max}(W_h)=1$. The additive state path in M2 changes that recurrence.

**Spine check.** Architecture-wise, a vanilla RNN compresses the prefix into $h_t$ and reuses the same $W_h$ at every step. Probability-wise, the output remains a causal conditional trained by teacher forcing; the main failure is the product of recurrent Jacobians and the resulting mismatch between observed and generated prefixes.
