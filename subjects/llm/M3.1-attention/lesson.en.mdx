## 1. Motivation

M2 attention read one encoder state sequence at a time. A Transformer performs the same normalized read for every position in parallel: queries decide which keys matter, and values are mixed by the resulting weights. The operation is not a fixed matrix because the weights depend on the current input. A decoder-only Transformer adds a causal mask so position $i$ can use positions $j\le i$ but cannot use future tokens.

This lesson derives the complete single-head backward pass, explains why logits are divided by $\sqrt{d_k}$, and separates serial dependency depth from arithmetic work. Multi-head attention then runs several lower-width heads and mixes their outputs with one output projection.

## 2. Forward definition

Let $X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$ be a sequence of $n$ residual-stream vectors. For one head, let

$$
W_Q\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_k},\qquad
W_K\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_k},\qquad
W_V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_v}.
$$

The projections, scores, causal mask, weights, and output are

$$
Q=XW_Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_k},\qquad
K=XW_K\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_k},\qquad
V=XW_V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_v},
$$

$$
S=\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},
$$

$$
\widetilde S_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
S_{ij},&j\le i,\\
-\infty,&j>i,
\end{cases}
\qquad
A=\operatorname{softmax}_{\mathrm{rows}}(\widetilde S)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},
$$

$$
Y=AV\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_v}.
$$

The mask is applied before the row softmax. A multi-head layer with $h$ heads and model width $d$ uses $d_h=d/h$ and concatenates $Y^{(1)},\ldots,Y^{(h)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_h}$:

$$
\operatorname{MHA}(X)=
\operatorname{Concat}(Y^{(1)},\ldots,Y^{(h)})W_O\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d},
\qquad
W_O\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.
$$

The row-stochastic matrix $A$ is an internal computation. The target probability remains the causal conditional emitted by the later output head.

**Source note.** The scaled dot-product, masking, multi-head definition, and complexity comparison follow Vaswani et al., “Attention Is All You Need” (https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/7181-attention-is-all-you-need and https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762).

## 3. Derivations

### D-M3.1-01 · Why divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$

**Theorem.** If $q,k\in\mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ have independent, zero-mean, unit-variance components and the corresponding components are independent, then

$$
\operatorname{Var}(q^\top k)=d_k,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{q^\top k}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)=1.
$$

**Steps.**

1. Expand the dot product:
   $$
   q^\top k=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_k}q_\ell k_\ell\in\mathbb{R}.
   $$
   Each product has mean $\mathbb{E}[q_\ell k_\ell]=\mathbb{E}[q_\ell]\mathbb{E}[k_\ell]=0$.
2. Its second moment is
   $$
   \mathbb{E}[(q_\ell k_\ell)^2]
   =\mathbb{E}[q_\ell^2]\mathbb{E}[k_\ell^2]
   =1.
   $$
   Since the product mean is zero, $\operatorname{Var}(q_\ell k_\ell)=1$.
3. Independence across coordinates makes cross-covariances vanish:
   $$
   \operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_k}q_\ell k_\ell\right)
   =\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_k}\operatorname{Var}(q_\ell k_\ell)
   =d_k.
   $$
4. For a scalar constant $a$, $\operatorname{Var}(aZ)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(Z)$. Set $a=d_k^{-1/2}$:
   $$
   \operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{q^\top k}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)
   =\frac{1}{d_k}d_k=1.
   $$
5. Without the scale, increasing $d_k$ spreads the score distribution. For a row vector $s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$, the softmax Jacobian from D-M0.1-01 is
   $$
   J_{\mathrm{softmax}}(a)=\operatorname{diag}(a)-aa^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.
   $$
   If one logit dominates, $a$ approaches a one-hot vector and every entry of this Jacobian approaches zero. Thus the larger unscaled variance can saturate the row softmax and kill its local gradient.
6. The numerical check samples dimensions $d_k\in\{4,16,64,256\}$, estimates both variances, and compares entropy with and without scaling. It obtains relative variance errors below $0.016$ and higher entropy for the scaled $d_k=256$ row.

**Check.** checks/M3/D-M3.1-01.py reports the empirical variance and entropy table and prints passed = True.

**Intuition.** The square-root factor keeps the typical score gap at an activation scale that does not grow with head width. It does not force every trained logit to have variance one; it controls the initialization-scale mechanism in this theorem.

### D-M3.1-02 · Full gradient through attention

**Theorem.** For an unprojected head $Y=AV$ and upstream $G=\nabla_YL\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_v}$,

$$
\nabla_VL=A^\top G\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_v},
\qquad
\nabla_AL=GV^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.
$$

For each row $i$, with $a_i=A_{i,:}^\top$ and $g_{A,i}=(\nabla_AL)_{i,:}^\top$,

$$
g_{S,i}=
\left(\operatorname{diag}(a_i)-a_ia_i^\top\right)g_{A,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
$$

Stacking rows and applying $S=QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k}$ gives

$$
\nabla_QL=\frac{\nabla_SL\,K}{\sqrt{d_k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_k},
\qquad
\nabla_KL=\frac{(\nabla_SL)^\top Q}{\sqrt{d_k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_k}.
$$

The projection gradients are

$$
\nabla_{W_Q}L=X^\top\nabla_QL\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_k},\quad
\nabla_{W_K}L=X^\top\nabla_KL\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_k},\quad
\nabla_{W_V}L=X^\top\nabla_VL\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_v}.
$$

At a masked position, the score gradient is exactly zero.

**Steps.**

1. Write the output differential:
   $$
   dY=dA\,V+A\,dV\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_v}.
   $$
   The scalar loss differential is
   $$
   dL=\langle G,dY\rangle_F
   =\langle G,dA\,V\rangle_F+\langle G,A\,dV\rangle_F.
   $$
2. Use the Frobenius trace identity on the second term:
   $$
   \langle G,A\,dV\rangle_F
   =\operatorname{tr}(G^\top A\,dV)
   =\operatorname{tr}((A^\top G)^\top dV).
   $$
   Therefore $\nabla_VL=A^\top G$.
3. Apply the same identity to the first term:
   $$
   \langle G,dA\,V\rangle_F
   =\operatorname{tr}(G^\top dA\,V)
   =\operatorname{tr}((GV^\top)^\top dA).
   $$
   Therefore $\nabla_AL=GV^\top$.
4. Row $i$ uses $a_i=\operatorname{softmax}(\widetilde s_i)$. Its differential is $da_i=J_i\,d\widetilde s_i$, where
   $$
   J_i=\operatorname{diag}(a_i)-a_ia_i^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.
   $$
   Its scalar contribution is $g_{A,i}^\top da_i=(J_i^\top g_{A,i})^\top d\widetilde s_i$. The Jacobian is symmetric, giving the displayed row formula.
5. A masked coordinate is a constant $-\infty$ input to the softmax. It has no differential, so $d\widetilde s_{ij}=0$ there and $(\nabla_SL)_{ij}=0$. This is why the implementation explicitly zeroes masked score gradients after the row-Jacobian multiplication.
6. For $S=QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k}$,
   $$
   dS=\frac{dQ\,K^\top+Q\,dK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}.
   $$
   The first term identifies $\nabla_QL=(\nabla_SL)K/\sqrt{d_k}$; transposing the second identifies $\nabla_KL=(\nabla_SL)^\top Q/\sqrt{d_k}$.
7. Finally, $Q=XW_Q$, $K=XW_K$, and $V=XW_V$. Apply the same matrix differential identity to obtain the three projection gradients and add their contributions to $\nabla_XL$.
8. The check uses $n=4,d=d_k=d_v=3$ and the scalar objective $\sum_{ij}Y_{ij}G_{ij}$. The common float64 harness reports maximum absolute error $9.472506112828682\times10^{-12}$ over $X,W_Q,W_K,W_V$. If PyTorch is installed, the same check compares these arrays to torch.float64 autograd; the current minimal environment reports torch_autograd = unavailable rather than inventing an autograd result.

**Check.** checks/M3/D-M3.1-02.py verifies all hand-written gradients, masked score gradients, and the optional autograd comparison; passed = True.

**Intuition.** The backward pass follows the forward dataflow in reverse: values, then row competition, then query/key geometry, then the three input projections. Causal masking removes a path rather than merely making it small.

### D-M3.1-03 · Attention as Nadaraya--Watson kernel regression

**Theorem.** Define

$$
\kappa(q_i,k_j)=\exp\left(\frac{q_i^\top k_j}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)>0.
$$

Then the attention row is the normalized kernel

$$
A_{ij}=\frac{\kappa(q_i,k_j)}{\sum_{r=1}^{n}\kappa(q_i,k_r)},
$$

and the output is

$$
y_i=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}v_j
=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\kappa(q_i,k_j)v_j}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\kappa(q_i,k_j)}
\in\mathbb{R}^{d_v}.
$$

**Steps.**

1. Start with the unmasked score $s_{ij}=q_i^\top k_j/\sqrt{d_k}\in\mathbb{R}$.
2. Exponentiating gives the positive kernel $\kappa(q_i,k_j)=e^{s_{ij}}$.
3. Row softmax divides each kernel value by the row sum, producing the displayed $A_{ij}$ and $\sum_jA_{ij}=1$.
4. Multiply the normalized weights by the value vectors and sum. The resulting expression is exactly the Nadaraya--Watson estimator, a differentiable dictionary lookup in which the query chooses the interpolation weights.
5. For fixed $Q,K$, $V\mapsto AV$ is linear. But $A$ changes with the input-derived $Q,K$, so the entire attention layer is a data-dependent linear map rather than one fixed MLP matrix.
6. The numerical check evaluates both forms on the same random arrays and verifies the linearity in $V$ at fixed weights to below $3\times10^{-17}$.

**Check.** checks/M3/D-M3.1-03 compares the kernel quotient to the attention output and prints passed = True.

**Analogy, labeled.** Calling attention a “soft dictionary lookup” is an analogy for the normalized kernel computation. The exact theorem is the equality to the Nadaraya--Watson quotient above.

### D-M3.1-04 · Causal masking before versus after softmax

**Theorem.** For a row with valid index set $\mathcal{V}$, adding $-\infty$ to invalid logits before softmax gives the same row as softmaxing all logits, setting invalid weights to zero, and renormalizing over $\mathcal{V}$:

$$
\frac{\exp(\widetilde s_j)}{\sum_{r\in\mathcal{V}}\exp(\widetilde s_r)}
=\frac{\exp(s_j)}{\sum_{r\in\mathcal{V}}\exp(s_r)}
\quad(j\in\mathcal{V}),
$$

with both sides zero for $j\notin\mathcal{V}$.

**Steps.**

1. For $j\in\mathcal{V}$, the pre-mask value is $\widetilde s_j=s_j$; for $j\notin\mathcal{V}$, $\exp(\widetilde s_j)=\exp(-\infty)=0$.
2. Therefore the denominator of the pre-masked softmax is
   $$
   \sum_{r=1}^{n}\exp(\widetilde s_r)
   =\sum_{r\in\mathcal{V}}\exp(s_r).
   $$
3. The valid numerator and denominator match the post-softmax-renormalized expression. Invalid coordinates are zero in both constructions.
4. Implementations use the pre-softmax form because one stable row-softmax pass preserves normalization and the mask contributes zero gradient automatically. A fully masked row is a separate empty-set edge case, not a normal causal row.
5. The check compares both algorithms on five random rows with an upper-triangular causal mask; the maximum difference is $1.1102230246251565\times10^{-16}$.

**Check.** checks/M3/D-M3.1-04 verifies equivalence, valid-row sums, and prints passed = True.

**Intuition.** The two procedures differ in when they remove invalid support, not in the final normalized distribution. Pre-softmax masking makes the intended support part of the softmax operation itself.

### D-M3.1-05 · Multi-head parameters, FLOPs, and dependency paths

**Claim.** With model width $d$, $h$ heads, and $d_h=d/h$, the four dense projection matrices $W_Q,W_K,W_V,W_O\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ contain

$$
4d^2
$$

parameters, ignoring biases. For sequence length $n$, the leading per-layer FLOP estimate in the course convention is

$$
8nd^2\quad\text{(Q/K/V/O projections)}
$$

plus

$$
4n^2d\quad\text{(score products and value products)}
$$

plus

$$
16nd^2\quad\text{(a $d\to4d\to d$ FFN)},
$$

so

$$
\operatorname{FLOPs}_{\mathrm{layer}}\approx24nd^2+4n^2d.
$$

**Steps.**

1. Each of Q, K, V maps $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ to $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ after concatenating head widths, so each contains $d^2$ entries. The output mixing matrix adds a fourth $d^2$ block.
2. A dense sequence projection uses $n d^2$ multiply-accumulates. Counting a multiply and an add as two FLOPs gives $2nd^2$ per projection and $8nd^2$ for four projections.
3. Across all heads, the score products and value products each cost approximately $2n^2d$ FLOPs in the same convention. Together they contribute $4n^2d$.
4. The FFN first expands $d$ to $4d$, then contracts $4d$ to $d$. The two matrix products contain $4d^2+4d^2=8d^2$ MACs per token, or $16nd^2$ FLOPs.
5. Add the three terms to obtain $24nd^2+4n^2d$. The check uses $d=12,n=5$ and obtains $4d^2=576$ and the stated expression.
6. The dependency table distinguishes three notions:

| Layer | Sequential operations | Longest path | Per-layer work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self-attention | $O(1)$ during training | $O(1)$ | $O(n^2d+nd^2)$ |
| RNN | $O(n)$ | $O(n)$ | $O(nd^2)$ |
| Convolution of width $k$ | $O(\log_k n)$ with a tree schedule | $O(\log_k n)$ | $O(knd^2)$ |

   Self-attention has quadratic pairwise work but constant training-time dependency depth; an RNN has linear dependency depth but no pairwise $n^2$ score matrix.

**Check.** checks/M3/D-M3.1-05 verifies the $4d^2$ count, FLOP decomposition, output shapes, and the complexity table; passed = True.

**Source note.** The sequential-operation and path-length comparison is the table-level comparison in Vaswani et al. 2017; the FLOP accounting above states the simplifying $4d$ FFN and multiply/add convention used by this course.

**Intuition.** Multi-head attention trades one wide similarity space for several narrower views. The model gains parallel, short paths at the price of an $n^2$ interaction term; later M4 will derive ways to reduce the memory traffic or cache cost.

## 6. Closing

**Counterintuitive question.** Why does self-attention have a shorter dependency path than an RNN even though it may do more arithmetic? **Worked answer:** D-M3.1-05 separates path length from work. During training, every position can form its query-key scores and value mix in parallel, so an information path from one position to another crosses one attention matrix. The matrix has $n^2$ pairwise entries, however, while an RNN performs $n$ sequential state transitions with $O(nd^2)$ work. A shorter path is a dependency statement, not a claim of lower total FLOPs or lower memory traffic.
