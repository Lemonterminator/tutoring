## 1. Motivation

M7.1 showed that a sequence mixer can be classified by a structured matrix. Positional encoding supplies another structure: translation acts on feature space. The useful question is not merely “where is token $i$?” It is “which representation of a position shift is applied before the query-key inner product?”

This view separates exact group identities from spectral and long-context approximations. RoPE gives an exact homomorphism for the rotations it defines. Bochner's theorem classifies shift-invariant positive-definite kernels. YaRN changes the usable frequency support through a context-extension procedure; it is not the same theorem as RoPE. ALiBi and T5 biases are relative-position Toeplitz logit corrections, not unitary representations.

## 2. Forward definition

Let the feature width be even, $d=2m$, and let frequencies $\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_m\in\mathbb{R}$. Define

$$
R(n)=\operatorname{blockdiag}_{r=1}^{m}
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos(n\omega_r)&-\sin(n\omega_r)\\
\sin(n\omega_r)&\cos(n\omega_r)
\end{bmatrix}
\in SO(d),
\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.
$$

For $q,k\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, rotary attention uses $R(m)q$ and $R(n)k$. A finite real spectral measure has atoms $(\omega_r,w_r)$ with $w_r\ge0$ and positional kernel

$$
\kappa(\delta)=\sum_{r=1}^{m}w_r\cos(\omega_r\delta),
\qquad \delta\in\mathbb{Z}.
$$

The [RoFormer paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864) gives the rotation and relative-position construction. The spectral statement below is the finite real form of Bochner's correspondence.

## 3. Derivations

### D-M7.4-01 · RoPE homomorphism and the spectral measure

**Theorem.** The map $n\mapsto R(n)$ is a group homomorphism from $(\mathbb{Z},+)$ into $SO(d)$:

$$
R(m)R(n)=R(m+n),
\qquad R(n)^\top R(n)=I_d,
\qquad \det R(n)=1.
$$

**Steps.**

1. For one frequency, multiply the two $2\times2$ blocks. The upper-left entry is
   $$
   \cos(m\omega)\cos(n\omega)-\sin(m\omega)\sin(n\omega)
   =\cos((m+n)\omega),
   $$
   and the off-diagonal entries are the corresponding sine addition identities. Therefore $R_2(m)R_2(n)=R_2(m+n)$.
2. Block-diagonal multiplication preserves this identity across all $m=d/2$ blocks, proving $R(m)R(n)=R(m+n)$. Each block has orthonormal columns and determinant one, so the full matrix lies in $SO(d)$.
3. Relative-position invariance follows from orthogonality:
   $$
   \langle R(m)q,R(n)k\rangle
   =q^\top R(m)^\top R(n)k
   =q^\top R(n-m)k.
   $$
   Absolute positions have been converted into a relative shift in the inner product.
4. For a real finite spectral measure $\nu=\sum_r w_r\delta_{\omega_r}$, define
   $$
   \kappa(\delta)=\int\cos(\omega\delta)\,d\nu(\omega)
   =\sum_r w_r\cos(\omega_r\delta).
   $$
   For positions $p_1,\ldots,p_N$, its Gram matrix has the feature representation
   $$
   K_{ij}=\kappa(p_i-p_j)
   =\sum_r w_r\bigl[\cos(\omega_r p_i)\cos(\omega_r p_j)+\sin(\omega_r p_i)\sin(\omega_r p_j)\bigr],
   $$
   so $K\succeq0$ when $w_r\ge0$. The converse classification for all translation-invariant positive-definite kernels is Bochner's theorem; the finite sum here is the explicit checkable special case.
5. YaRN changes the frequency allocation used by a RoPE model for a longer context. In spectral language, it reshapes the effective spectral measure. This is a sourced approximation/engineering procedure, not a new homomorphism proof; see [YaRN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071).
6. ALiBi adds a bias such as $B_{ij}=-m_h|i-j|$, and T5 uses relative-position buckets. Both have constant diagonals, so they are Toeplitz logit corrections in the sequence index. They modify attention logits rather than rotating feature vectors. Sources: [ALiBi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409) and [T5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683).
7. Fourier features and neural tangent spectral bias explain why frequency allocation affects which variation is easy to fit; this is a modeling perspective, not a claim that one finite RoPE grid is optimal. [Fourier Features](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10739) provides the spectral-feature context. [LieRE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10322) generalizes the construction to learned Lie-group generators for higher-dimensional positions; its generator exponential is a group-representation generalization, not the same one-dimensional $\mathbb{Z}$ proof.
8. The float64 check verifies the homomorphism, $SO(d)$ constraints, relative-inner-product identity, positive semidefiniteness of the finite spectral Gram matrix, and Toeplitz diagonals for ALiBi/T5-style corrections.

## 6. Closing

**Counterintuitive question.** Why can a relative-position kernel be positive definite even though its entries oscillate and become negative?

**Worked answer.** Positive definiteness is a matrix property, not entrywise nonnegativity. D-M7.4-01 writes the Gram matrix as a sum of outer products of cosine/sine feature vectors with nonnegative weights. Individual entries $\cos(\omega\delta)$ can be negative, while every quadratic form $a^\top K a$ remains nonnegative. This is the finite spectral-measure form of Bochner's theorem.
