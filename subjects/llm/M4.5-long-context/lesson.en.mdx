## 1. Motivation

M3.2 showed that RoPE rotates each pair of coordinates at a frequency. At positions longer than training, the phase range and attention distribution move out of the regime seen during training. A longer KV cache alone does not solve this defect.

M4.5 separates five fixes. Wavelength accounting tells us which planes are high or low frequency. Position interpolation and NTK-aware scaling change the phase schedule. YaRN ramps between them and changes attention temperature. Sliding windows and sinks change which positions receive mass. A sparse indexer selects a smaller key set before exact attention.

## 2. Forward definition

For even rotary dimension $d_R\in\mathbb{N}$ and base $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}>1$, RoPE plane $i$ has angular frequency

$$
\theta_i=\theta_{\mathrm{base}}^{-2i/d_R}>0,
\qquad
\theta_i\in\mathbb{R},
\qquad
i=0,\ldots,d_R/2-1.
$$

Its wavelength is

$$
\lambda_i=\frac{2\pi}{\theta_i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.
$$

Position interpolation with extension scale $s>0$ maps a position $p\in\mathbb{R}$ to

$$
p'=\frac{p}{s}\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

For YaRN, let $L_{\mathrm{train}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ be the original pretrained context length and $L_{\mathrm{target}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ the target length. The extension scale is

$$
s=\frac{L_{\mathrm{target}}}{L_{\mathrm{train}}}>0,
\qquad
r_i=\frac{L_{\mathrm{train}}}{\lambda_i}.
$$

With thresholds $\alpha=1,\beta=32$, define

$$
\gamma(r_i)=
\operatorname{clip}\left(\frac{r_i-\alpha}{\beta-\alpha},0,1\right)\in[0,1],
$$

$$
\theta'_i=(1-\gamma(r_i))\frac{\theta_i}{s}
+\gamma(r_i)\theta_i\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

The YaRN temperature curve is an empirical fit:

$$
a=0.1\ln s+1,
\qquad
t=\frac{1}{a^2}.
$$

The factor $a$ is applied to each query and key vector. Therefore the logit $q^\top k$ is multiplied by $a^2=1/t$.

**Sources.** RoPE is Su et al., [RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864). YaRN is Peng et al., [YaRN: Efficient Context Window Extension of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071). Attention sinks are Xiao et al., [Efficient Streaming Language Models with Attention Sinks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453). The DSA model-specific description is DeepSeek-AI, [DeepSeek-V3.2: Pushing the Frontier of Open Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556).

## 3. Derivations

### D-M4.5-01 · Wavelengths, position interpolation, and NTK-aware scaling

**Theorem, scoped to the RoPE formula.** The wavelength of plane $i$ is $2\pi/\theta_i$. Position interpolation divides positions by $s$, while a common NTK-aware convention changes the base; neither operation by itself proves task-level extrapolation.

**Steps.**

1. A RoPE plane rotates a two-vector by angle $p\theta_i$. One full cycle has angle $2\pi$. Set $p\theta_i=2\pi$ and solve for $p$:
   $$
   \lambda_i\theta_i=2\pi,
   \qquad
   \lambda_i=\frac{2\pi}{\theta_i}.
   $$
   The wavelength is a scalar in $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$.
2. For $\theta_i=\theta_{\mathrm{base}}^{-2i/d_R}$, substitute:
   $$
   \lambda_i
   =2\pi\theta_{\mathrm{base}}^{2i/d_R}.
   $$
   Higher plane index has a longer wavelength under this convention.
3. Position interpolation maps $p$ to $p'=p/s$. The new phase is
   $$
   p'\theta_i=\frac{p\theta_i}{s}.
   $$
   Thus every phase is slowed by the same factor, keeping the target position range closer to the original phase range. This is an algebraic phase transformation, not a proof that the model preserves all long-range behavior.
4. A common NTK-aware base-change convention is
   $$
   \theta_{\mathrm{base}}'
   =\theta_{\mathrm{base}}s^{d_R/(d_R-2)},
   \qquad
   \theta_{\mathrm{base}}>1,\ s>0,\ d_R>2.
   $$
   The exponent and implementation variants are a heuristic/engineering convention. The check only verifies that this convention increases the base for $s>1$.
5. The check evaluates $\lambda_i$, positions $[0,4,8]$ under $s=4$, and the base change at $d_R=8$. It obtains wavelengths $[6.283185307179586,62.83185307179586,628.3185307179587,6283.185307179586]$ for the four planes in the fixture.

**Check.** checks/M4/D-M4.5-01.py verifies the wavelength identity, position interpolation, and the stated NTK-aware convention; it prints passed: True.

**Approximation, labeled.** NTK-aware scaling is a family of heuristics, not a theorem of positional generalization. Its exact exponent and base schedule must be attributed to the chosen implementation.

### D-M4.5-02 · YaRN bands and attention temperature

**Claim, sourced and scoped.** YaRN uses the original pretrained length in $r_i=L_{\mathrm{train}}/\lambda_i$. Low-frequency dimensions receive full position interpolation, high-frequency dimensions remain unchanged, and intermediate dimensions use a smooth ramp. The physical explanation is the paper's interpretation, not a proven theorem.

**Steps.**

1. Start with the wavelength from D-M4.5-01 and define
   $$
   r_i=\frac{L_{\mathrm{train}}}{\lambda_i}.
   $$
   $r_i$ counts how many wavelengths fit into the original training context up to a $2\pi$ convention. Crucially, the numerator is $L_{\mathrm{train}}$, not $L_{\mathrm{target}}$.
2. Define the ramp:
   $$
   \gamma(r_i)=
   \begin{cases}
   0,&r_i<\alpha,\\
   (r_i-\alpha)/(\beta-\alpha),&\alpha\le r_i\le\beta,\\
   1,&r_i>\beta.
   \end{cases}
   $$
   For LLaMA-tuned values $\alpha=1,\beta=32$, the compact clip expression in Section 2 gives the same three cases.
3. Interpolate the frequency:
   $$
   \theta'_i
   =(1-\gamma(r_i))\frac{\theta_i}{s}
   +\gamma(r_i)\theta_i.
   $$
   If $r_i<\alpha$, $\gamma=0$ and $\theta'_i=\theta_i/s$. If $r_i>\beta$, $\gamma=1$ and $\theta'_i=\theta_i$. Between them, the two frequencies mix continuously.
4. Using $L_{\mathrm{target}}$ in the ratio would instead give
   $$
   r_i^{\mathrm{wrong}}
   =\frac{L_{\mathrm{target}}}{\lambda_i}
   =s\frac{L_{\mathrm{train}}}{\lambda_i}
   =sr_i.
   $$
   Therefore the thresholds are effectively shifted to $\alpha/s$ and $\beta/s$, misclassifying the bands. This is the version-independent implementation trap.
5. The paper's physical motivation is that high-frequency dimensions complete many rotations in the training context and mainly encode relative position, while low-frequency dimensions have not completed a full rotation and mainly encode absolute position. The lesson labels this as an interpretation, not a theorem.
6. YaRN defines an empirical curve
   $$
   a=0.1\ln s+1.
   $$
   The factor $a$ is applied separately:
   $$
   q'=aq,\qquad k'=ak,
   \qquad
   q,k,q',k'\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
   $$
   Therefore
   $$
   (q')^\top k'
   =(aq)^\top(ak)
   =a^2q^\top k.
   $$
7. Define $t=1/a^2$. Dividing logits by $t$ is equivalent:
   $$
   \frac{(q')^\top k'}{\sqrt d}
   =\frac{q^\top k}{t\sqrt d}.
   $$
   For $s>1$, $a>1$, so $t<1$ and the softmax sharpens. It does not soften. The paper's entropy motivation is empirical/qualitative; later analyses may give different explanations, but no theorem is claimed here.
8. There is a version trap. YaRN arXiv v1 used the reciprocal convention for its $t$ compared with the v2/ICLR convention. The symbols look contradictory unless the version is recorded. This lesson uses $t=1/(0.1\ln s+1)^2$ and states that convention explicitly.
9. The check uses $L_{\mathrm{train}}=2\pi$, frequencies $[0.5,2,64]$, and $s=4$. It obtains ratios $[0.5,2,64]$, ramp values $[0,1/31,1]$, $a=1.138629436111989$, logit factor $a^2=1.2964769927807063$, and $t=0.7713210535693215$.

**Check.** checks/M4/D-M4.5-02.py verifies the original-length ratio, low/ramp/high bands, and the squared temperature factor; it prints passed: True.

**Approximation, labeled.** YaRN's band interpretation and temperature curve are empirical design choices. The interpolation algebra and the $a^2$ logit factor are exact once the convention is selected.

### D-M4.5-03 · Sliding-window attention

**Definition.** With causal window width $w\in\mathbb{N}$, query position $i$ can attend to

$$
\mathcal{W}(i)
=\{\max(0,i-w+1),\ldots,i\}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,n-1\}.
$$

The per-layer attention work is $O(nwd)$ rather than $O(n^2d)$.

**Steps.**

1. Causality excludes positions $j>i$. A width-$w$ window also excludes positions $j<i-w+1$. Intersecting both conditions gives $\mathcal{W}(i)$.
2. For interior positions, the set contains $w$ keys. Across $n$ queries, there are $O(nw)$ score pairs. Each score/value operation has width $d$, so the leading arithmetic is
   $$
   F_{\mathrm{window}}=O(nwd).
   $$
3. A token can move information through at most $w$ earlier positions per layer. In a coarse no-skip model, a dependency can grow by $w$ per layer, giving receptive-field scale $O(Lw)$ after $L$ layers.
4. Interleaving a full-attention layer changes this bound qualitatively: the full layer supplies a global path. The exact receptive field then depends on layer placement, not only on $Lw$.
5. The check obtains window indices $[2,3,4,5]$ for query 5 and width 4, a three-layer coarse field of 12, and an unbounded marker when a full layer is interleaved in the fixture.

**Check.** checks/M4/D-M4.5-03.py verifies the causal window and the stated receptive-field accounting; it prints passed: True.

**Approximation, labeled.** $O(Lw)$ is a path-length estimate. It does not prove that every task dependency is preserved or that a full layer always yields useful information.

### D-M4.5-04 · Attention sinks

**Theorem, scoped to softmax normalization.** If a softmax has no null option, its probability mass must be assigned to one of the available keys. Adding a learned sink logit gives the denominator an explicit placeholder option whose mass can later be dropped from the value output.

**Steps.**

1. For token scores $s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$, ordinary softmax is
   $$
   p_j=\frac{e^{s_j}}{\sum_{r=1}^{n}e^{s_r}},
   \qquad
   p\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
   \qquad
   \sum_{j=1}^{n}p_j=1.
   $$
   There is no coordinate for “attend nowhere.” Even a semantically useless early token must receive some mass.
2. Add a scalar sink logit $s_{\mathrm{sink}}\in\mathbb{R}$ to form
   $$
   \widetilde s=[s_1,\ldots,s_n,s_{\mathrm{sink}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.
   $$
   Its softmax has token probabilities $\widetilde p_{1:n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and sink mass $\widetilde p_{\mathrm{sink}}\in\mathbb{R}$ satisfying
   $$
   \sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde p_j+\widetilde p_{\mathrm{sink}}=1.
   $$
3. The value output uses only token values $V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_v}$:
   $$
   y=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde p_jv_j\in\mathbb{R}^{d_v}.
   $$
   The sink has no value row; its mass is deliberately discarded after normalization.
4. With two equal token scores $[-100,-100]$ and sink logit $4$, the check obtains sink mass $1.0$ to float64 display precision and remaining token mass $1.3627113643090597\times10^{-45}$. Without an explicit sink, equal token scores split mass $[0.5,0.5]$.
5. StreamingLLM reports the empirical attention-sink phenomenon and uses initial tokens or a dedicated sink to stabilize a finite cache. The observation and deployment method are sourced claims; the normalization algebra above is exact.

**Check.** checks/M4/D-M4.5-04.py verifies no-null softmax mass and the explicit sink denominator; it prints passed: True.

**Claim, sourced and scoped.** A sink is a learned or designated mass-absorbing option. It is not evidence that the sink token is semantically important; its role is to make the normalization have a place for otherwise unusable mass.

### D-M4.5-05 · Sparse attention with an indexer

**Definition, scoped to DSA-style selection.** A cheap indexer scores keys, selects top-$k$ indices, and runs exact attention on the selected set. If $q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, keys $K\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$, values $V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_v}$, and $W_I\in\mathbb{R}^{d_I\times d}$, a simple index score is

$$
\sigma_j=(W_Iq)^\top(W_Ik_j)\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

Selecting $k$ keys changes the score/value work to $O(nkd)$ for the selected attention path.

**Steps.**

1. Project the query and keys into the cheap indexer space:
   $$
   q_I=W_Iq\in\mathbb{R}^{d_I},
   \qquad
   K_I=KW_I^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_I}.
   $$
2. Compute index scores:
   $$
   \sigma=K_Iq_I\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
   $$
   Let $J(q)$ be the indices of the $k$ largest coordinates. The top-k operation is discrete:
   $$
   J(q)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},
   \qquad
   |J(q)|=k.
   $$
3. Run exact attention on selected keys and values:
   $$
   s_J=K_{J(q)}q\in\mathbb{R}^{k},
   \qquad
   \alpha_J=\operatorname{softmax}(s_J)\in\mathbb{R}^{k},
   $$
   $$
   y=\alpha_J^\top V_{J(q)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_v}.
   $$
4. Conditional on a fixed $J(q)$, the selected attention calculation is differentiable in $q,K,V$. The index selection itself is not differentiable through the top-k boundary. The indexer scores can be trained only through a surrogate, straight-through rule, auxiliary objective, or another explicitly chosen estimator; no such estimator is silently assumed.
5. The check selects keys $[0,2]$, obtains normalized selected weights $[0.5498339973124778,0.4501660026875221]$, and verifies that a small query perturbation changes the conditional attention output while the discrete selected mask remains fixed.
6. DeepSeek-V3.2's DSA description is a model-specific source claim. The widget and check implement a minimal indexer abstraction to make the differentiability boundary visible; they do not claim to reproduce the complete production DSA system.

**Check.** checks/M4/D-M4.5-05.py verifies top-k selection and the distinction between a fixed-mask differentiable path and a discrete selection path; it prints passed: True.

**Claim, sourced and scoped.** Sparse attention reduces work only if the selected set is small and the indexer overhead is cheaper than dense attention. The selection mechanism can lose keys; performance and quality must be measured, not inferred from the asymptotic expression alone.

## 6. Closing

**Counterintuitive question.** Why does increasing the target context length make YaRN's attention temperature smaller, even though the per-vector factor $a$ becomes larger?

**Worked answer.** D-M4.5-02 defines $a=0.1\ln s+1$. YaRN applies $a$ to both query and key:

$$
q'=aq,\qquad k'=ak.
$$

The logit contains their dot product:

$$
(q')^\top k'=a^2q^\top k.
$$

Writing the same operation as attention temperature gives $t=1/a^2$. For $s>1$, $a>1$ and $t<1$, so the softmax sharpens. Saying “the logits multiply by $a$” drops one of the two vector factors and reverses the quantitative conclusion.
