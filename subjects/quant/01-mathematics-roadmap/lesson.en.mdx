## Suggested sequence

For the courses currently in the graph, use this navigation order:

1. **MIT 18.06SC Linear Algebra**: review systems, eigenvalues, projections and SVD through problems.
2. **MIT 18.02SC Multivariable Calculus**: review gradients, Hessians, multiple integrals and changes of variables.
3. **MIT 6.041SC Applied Probability**: conditional probability, joint distributions, conditional expectation and limit theorems.
4. **Aalto MS-C1620 Statistical Inference (Chinese translation)**: estimation, tests, regression and diagnostics.
5. **MIT 18.650 Statistical Inference**: deepen likelihood, asymptotic theory, Bayesian methods and statistical models; enter directly if step 4 is already familiar.
6. **MIT 18.065 Matrix Methods for Data Analysis**: matrix factorizations, least squares, PCA and regularization.
7. **Aalto MS-C2105 Introduction to Optimization**: objectives, constraints and basic solution methods.
8. **Aalto ELEC-E5424 Convex Optimization**: convexity, duality and KKT; then practice portfolio optimization.
9. **Aalto MS-C2111 Stochastic Processes**: Markov chains and evolving random models.
10. **Aalto MS-E1600 Measure-Theoretic Probability**: convergence, conditional expectation and theoretical conditions for stochastic analysis.
11. **Aalto MS-E1604 Brownian Motion and Stochastic Analysis**: martingales, Itô integration and SDEs.
12. **Aalto ELEC-E8425 Energy Systems Modeling and Optimization**: domain practice in optimization and stochastic modeling.
13. **Aalto ELEC-E8125 Reinforcement Learning**: sequential decision-making after probability, optimization and Markov models.

Review the first two through problems. Choose the depth of steps 4–5 according to mastery; steps 7–8 and step 9 can run in parallel. Prediction research should add **time series, chronological validation and risk evaluation** after inference. Pricing should prioritize steps 9–11, then numerical methods and financial pricing. Numbers describe the recommended navigation route, rather than strict prerequisites between every adjacent course.

This is a study recommendation. The [MIT 18.650 syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-650-statistics-for-applications-fall-2016/pages/syllabus/) specifies probability theory and some linear algebra; the [MIT Analytics of Finance syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-450-analytics-of-finance-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/) also assumes calculus, probability and statistics. These requirements support studying probability and statistics before financial modeling.

For **prediction and quantitative research**, first strengthen linear algebra/calculus and probability/statistics; then study regression, time series, chronological validation, model evaluation and portfolio optimization. The new regression-to-risk reading fits after basic regression and distributions. Reinforcement learning is a later specialization once evaluation assumptions are understood.

For **derivatives pricing and financial modeling**, strengthen analysis and probability; then study martingales, stochastic calculus, numerical simulation/PDEs, and financial pricing/hedging. Statistical evaluation remains useful, while the continuous-time branch deserves more emphasis.

The **Black–Scholes pricing track** now has four connected courses. Start with <a href={quantLessonHref('QUANT-P1')}>numerical foundations</a> before refactoring code, continue to <a href={quantLessonHref('QUANT-P2')}>pricing, parity and Greeks</a>, study <a href={quantLessonHref('QUANT-P3')}>implied volatility and root finding</a> before implementing IV, and finish with <a href={quantLessonHref('QUANT-P4')}>C++ numerical engineering</a>. Each course contains source-checked reading, derivations and exercises tied to the Delta/Gamma assignment and library plan. The numerical course corrects the earlier review of the assignment's 45° Gamma formula.

For **portfolio risk**, study covariance estimation and dependence alongside marginal forecasting. Read the joint-calibration counterexample before concluding that individually calibrated forecasts imply correct portfolio risk.
