This section involves bumping parameters on a known price function to compute Greeks; it differs from solving the BS PDE using a finite difference grid.

## 1. Start from the Same Taylor Table

Fix other parameters and denote $f(x)=C(x)$. In a smooth region, write

$$
f(x+jh)=f+jhf'+\frac{j^2h^2}{2}f''+\frac{j^3h^3}{6}f^{(3)}+\frac{j^4h^4}{24}f^{(4)}+O(h^5).
$$

All derivatives on the right side are evaluated at $x$. Taking $j=1,2,-1$ yields the two forward formulas from the original assignment and the common central formula. $f^{(k)}$ denotes the k-th derivative.

## 2. First-Order Differences

Forward difference subtracts first then divides by $h$:

$$
D_f(h)=\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h=f'+\frac h2 f''+\frac{h^2}6 f^{(3)}+O(h^3).
$$

The leading error is $O(h)$, so when the step size is halved, the error should approximately halve in the truncation-dominated region. The odd-symmetric subtraction in central difference cancels even terms:

$$
D_c(h)=\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}=f'+\frac{h^2}6 f^{(3)}+O(h^4).
$$

Its leading error is $O(h^2)$. Higher orders require better symmetry or weight combinations, not automatically obtained merely by "using more price points".

## 3. The Gamma in the original assignment is a forward three-point

Note that it is not a central second-order difference. Multiply the three rows of $j=2,1,0$ by $1,-2,1$ respectively:

$$
G_f(h)=\frac{f(x+2h)-2f(x+h)+f(x)}{h^2}=f''+h f^{(3)}+\frac{7h^2}{12}f^{(4)}+O(h^3).
$$

The `gamma_forward` in the original assignment is $O(h)$. The central second-order difference is

$$
G_c(h)=\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}=f''+\frac{h^2}{12}f^{(4)}+O(h^4).
$$

## 4. How does floating-point subtraction amplify errors?

Let each calculated price be $\widehat f_j=f(x+jh)+\eta_j$, where $|\eta_j|\le\epsilon M$. $M$ is the absolute error scale of the price calculation, which is not necessarily equal to the small final price; for deep ITM/OTM cases or when terms in the pricing formula cancel out, consider the errors in intermediate terms and the pricing algorithm.

The error perturbation for forward Delta is $(\eta_1-\eta_0)/h$, with an absolute upper bound of $2\epsilon M/h$. The perturbation for forward Gamma is $(\eta_2-2\eta_1+\eta_0)/h^2$, with an upper bound of $4\epsilon M/h^2$.

$$
E(h)\lesssim a h^p+\frac{b\epsilon M}{h^k}.
$$

Here $p$ is the truncation order, and $k$ is the differentiation order. The first term decreases as $h$ becomes smaller, while the second term increases. Additionally, $x+h$ may be rounded to $x$, causing the bump to disappear.

In float64, `eps` is approximately $2.22\times10^{-16}$, and the unit roundoff for nearest rounding is half of that. The following analysis considers only the order of magnitude, denoted as $\epsilon$, and does not conflate the two concepts into exact constants.

Differentiating the error model and setting the derivative to zero yields

$$
h_*^{p+k}=\frac{k b\epsilon M}{p a}.
$$

## 5. Determine entry into the asymptotic regime using a sequence

Let $h_n=h_0/2^n$, $E_n=|G(h_n)-\Gamma_{ref}|$. Define

$$
R_n=\frac{E_n}{E_{n+1}},\quad p_n=\frac{\log R_n}{\log 2}.
$$

If $E_n\sim c h_n^p$ and $c\ne0$, then $R_n\to2^p$: first order corresponds to 2, second order corresponds to 4, and fourth order corresponds to 16. This pattern breaks down after entering the roundoff-dominated regime. Do not blindly compute this ratio when the error is exactly 0, the reference is biased, or the leading coefficient is 0.

Richardson extrapolation is also derived from the same formula. If $G(h)=g+a h^p+O(h^{p+1})$, then

$$
G_R(h)=\frac{2^pG(h/2)-G(h)}{2^p-1}
$$

the leading error is eliminated. It also combines the noise from two computations, so it yields reliable benefits only in the truncation-dominated regime.

## 8. Extension: Placing Discrete Greeks Back into the BS Diffusion Equation

Previously, derivatives were taken on closed-form prices. If no closed-form solution exists, prices can still be computed over the entire Spot/maturity grid. Take a uniform Spot grid $S_i=i\,\delta S$, and march forward from the payoff at maturity $V_i^0$ along the remaining time to maturity $T$. Central differences directly yield grid Greeks:

$$
\Delta_i^n\approx\frac{V_{i+1}^n-V_{i-1}^n}{2\delta S},\quad\Gamma_i^n\approx\frac{V_{i+1}^n-2V_i^n+V_{i-1}^n}{(\delta S)^2}.
$$

Substitute into the $V_T$ equation from Part 01, and advance one step using explicit Euler:

$$
V_i^{n+1}=V_i^n+\delta T\left(\frac12\sigma^2S_i^2\Gamma_i^n+(r-q)S_i\Delta_i^n-rV_i^n\right).
$$

This connects the computation of Greeks and prices into the same discrete model: curvature drives diffusion, slope drives drift, and the price itself undergoes discounting. Rearranged as a weighted sum of three neighboring points:

$$
V_i^{n+1}=a_iV_{i-1}^n+b_iV_i^n+c_iV_{i+1}^n.
$$

$$
a_i=\frac{\delta T}{2}\left(\frac{\sigma^2S_i^2}{(\delta S)^2}-\frac{(r-q)S_i}{\delta S}\right).
$$

$$
b_i=1-\delta T\left(\frac{\sigma^2S_i^2}{(\delta S)^2}+r\right).
$$

$$
c_i=\frac{\delta T}{2}\left(\frac{\sigma^2S_i^2}{(\delta S)^2}+\frac{(r-q)S_i}{\delta S}\right).
$$

One can check $a_i+b_i+c_i=1-r\delta T$. In cases where $r\ge0$ and boundary conditions are handled correctly, requiring non-negative weights and controlling their sum is an easily understood sufficient condition for maximum norm stability. $b_i\ge0$ restricts the time step size at each point; $a_i,c_i\ge0$ also requires that the drift is not too strong relative to grid diffusion. Simply reducing the time step size does not automatically resolve negative weights at all spatial points.
