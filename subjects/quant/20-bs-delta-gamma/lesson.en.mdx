Goal: Derive analytical Delta/Gamma by hand and understand why numerical differentiation is particularly sensitive for short-dated ATM options. Duration: approximately 60 minutes. Prerequisite: Part 01.

## 1. Prove a cancellation identity before differentiating

The price is $C=A\Phi(d_1)-B\Phi(d_2)$, $A=Se^{-qT},B=Ke^{-rT}$. A long list of terms appears when differentiating, but not all of them remain. The key is

$$
A\phi(d_1)=B\phi(d_2).
$$

Why? Because $d_2=d_1-\tau$, $d_1\tau=m+\tau^2/2$, therefore

$$
d_1^2-d_2^2=2d_1\tau-\tau^2=2m.
$$

The density ratio is thus $\phi(d_1)/\phi(d_2)=e^{-m}=K/F$. And $A/B=F/K$, their product is 1.

**Paper pause A:** Without looking at the final result, expand $(d_1-\tau)^2$ and calculate the density ratio. Write the negative sign in the exponent clearly.

<summary>Pause A answer</summary>


$\phi(d_1)/\phi(d_2)=\exp(-(d_1^2-d_2^2)/2)=e^{-m}$, so $A\phi(d_1)=B\phi(d_2)$. This is a tool repeatedly used in the derivations of Delta, Vega, and Rho.


</details>

## 2. Delta: How do terms in the product rule vanish?

Take the partial derivative with respect to $S$, holding $K,r,q,\sigma,T$ fixed. $m_S=1/S$, $\tau_S=0$, so

$$
(d_1)_S=(d_2)_S=\frac1{S\tau}.
$$

Use $\Phi'=\phi$ and the product rule:

$$
C_S=e^{-qT}\Phi(d_1)+A\phi(d_1)(d_1)_S-B\phi(d_2)(d_2)_S.
$$

The density factors of the last two terms are equal, and the $S$ derivative of $d_1,d_2$ is also equal, hence

$$
\Delta_C=e^{-qT}\Phi(d_1).
$$

This is the first-order local slope. For a small price change $s$, $C(S+s)-C(S)\approx\Delta s$; it is not the exact payoff for a finite change, nor a direct prediction of the probability of future real gains or losses.

## 3. Gamma: Take the derivative again

$e^{-qT}$ is constant with respect to $S$, so we only need to differentiate the CDF:

$$
\Gamma_C=\Delta_S=e^{-qT}\phi(d_1)(d_1)_S=\frac{e^{-qT}\phi(d_1)}{S\sigma\sqrt{T}}.
$$

Gamma is positive, so the European Call is a convex function of $S$. Retain one more term in the Taylor price change:

$$
C(S+s)-C(S)=\Delta s+\frac12\Gamma s^2+O(s^3).
$$

If you short $\Delta$ units of the underlying to offset the linear term, what remains is the curvature term and changes in other factors. In the diffusion process $(dS)^2=\sigma^2S^2dt$, which exactly produces the $\sigma^2S^2\Gamma/2$ in the PDE.

## 4. Why is the second scenario dangerous?

Original assignment scenario 2: $S=K=100,r=q=0,\sigma=0.01,T=1/365$. Here $\tau\approx0.0005234239$, $d_1=\tau/2$, Gamma is approximately 7.6218, which is hundreds of times larger than in scenario 1.

At the zero carry ATM point, when $\tau\to0$, $d_1\to0$, therefore

$$
\Gamma\sim\frac1{S\tau\sqrt{2\pi}}.
$$

The width of the price smooth kink is approximately $L=S\tau$: about 20 in scenario 1, and about 0.05234 in scenario 2. The same absolute step size $h$ is larger relative to the local width in the second scenario, making higher-order Taylor terms harder to ignore.

This explosion conclusion is limited to the vicinity of the exercise boundary. With fixed $S/K\ne1$ and letting $T\to0$, $|d_1|$ typically tends to infinity, as the exponential decay of the density can overcome $1/\sqrt{T}$; Gamma does not explode simultaneously at all price locations.

## 5. To what extent does 'log-density to prevent underflow' go?

The original assignment suggested $\log\phi(d_1)=-d_1^2/2-\log\sqrt{2\pi}$. This helps compute the density and the proportionality factor together:

$$
\log\Gamma=-qT-\frac12d_1^2-\frac12\log(2\pi)-\log S-\log\sigma-\frac12\log T.
$$

Performing exp one final time avoids the intermediate loss caused by first computing the density as 0 and then multiplying by a large factor. However, if the final Gamma itself is too small to be represented in floating-point format, exp will still underflow; the logarithmic domain does not offer infinite precision.

Analytic closed-form formulas must also contend with CDF precision, exponential range, and cancellation issues. Therefore, when using them as a reference in experiments, it should be explicitly stated as a closed-form float64 comparison; estimating the error of the reference itself requires an independent high-precision implementation.
