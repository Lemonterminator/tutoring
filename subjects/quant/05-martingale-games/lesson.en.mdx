## Read the definition after playing

A discrete-time process $(M_n)$ is a martingale relative to a filtration $(\mathcal F_n)$ when all of these hold:

- **Adaptedness**: $M_n$ is known at time $n$, meaning it is $\mathcal F_n$-measurable.
- **Integrability**: $E[|M_n|]<\infty$ at every time.
- **Conditional mean preservation**: $E[M_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]=M_n$ almost surely.

The finite-valued, finite-horizon examples satisfy the first two conditions, allowing you to focus on the third. The martingale property depends on both the filtration and probability law. Changing either requires checking the property again.

## Remaining limits

Our four-leaf trees permit direct enumeration. This does not establish mastery of general conditional expectations, zero-probability events or continuous-time martingales. Square compensation practices one construction; stopping covers a bounded rule and a failure example rather than every version of optional stopping.

Next time avoid hints and explain why excluded branches cannot remain in a conditional average. In the final stage the first hitting time of $+1$ is almost surely finite but has infinite expectation. **Stopping almost surely** alone does not preserve the initial expected value.
